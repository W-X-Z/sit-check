# Camera and vision methods for measuring one person's seated posture more precisely than a frontal 2D webcam (notes as of Oct 2026)

> Source-access note: the session's egress policy blocked full-text pages on PMC/NCBI, MDPI, Springer, PLOS, Frontiers and arXiv (HTTP 403 at the proxy). The figures below come from search-engine extracts of those pages plus vendor and documentation pages. Each number is attributed to its paper, but table-level details such as per-DOF errors were often not visible. Those gaps are listed explicitly. "Validated" means a comparison against a reference (mocap, goniometer/inclinometer, Spinal Mouse, radiograph). "Demo/classifier" means accuracy of posture classes only, with no angle agreement.

## 1. Side-view (sagittal) camera: second webcam or iPhone (Continuity Camera) beside the desk, markerless 2D pose or stickers

### Takeaway
A lateral camera is the only single-camera setup that sees the sagittal variables directly: head-forward position, head flexion, trunk inclination, and, with stickers, a true tragus–C7 CVA and a shoulder-forward angle. Markerless models such as MediaPipe, Apple Vision and OpenPose have no tragus or C7 keypoint, though. Published markerless head and neck results show systematic bias and only moderate agreement (about 5–9° RMSE for neck and trunk). With a desk and chair in the way, thoracic kyphosis, lumbar curve and pelvic tilt are not observable. Tragus + C7 sticker photogrammetry is the validated route to CVA (pooled ICC about 0.89–0.90; MDC about 5°).

### Cited Findings
**Markerless 2D keypoints for head and neck from a side view**
- A comparative study of MediaPipe Pose for postural assessment against an optoelectronic reference found that the head-angle estimate showed a **significant systematic discrepancy in most markerless models**. The authors attribute this to landmark definition: the reference used a forehead marker, while the models estimate a differently positioned head point. The same paper defines a sagittal trunk/head angle where about 90° means upright. — [Deep Dive into MediaPipe Pose for Postural Assessment (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398607117_A_Deep_Dive_into_MediaPipe_Pose_for_Postural_Assessment_A_Comparative_Investigation)
- A related extract, which may be from the same group (Parkinson's axial posture, attribution unverified), reports three things. Lower-complexity MediaPipe models had high congruence with a 3D kinematic reference for trunk and shoulder alignment (ρ > 0.75). **A lateral camera view significantly improved tracking of sagittal angles (ρ ≥ 0.72).** The high-complexity model introduced skeletal distortions. — [same ResearchGate entry / search extract](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398607117_A_Deep_Dive_into_MediaPipe_Pose_for_Postural_Assessment_A_Comparative_Investigation)
- A 2024 study detecting forward head posture from 3D pose estimation (graph convolutional network) notes that **most pose models do not include the craniovertebral angle, which requires the C7 vertebra**. It reached only 78.27% *classification* accuracy (a demo/classifier, not angle validation). — [JMIR Form Res / PMC11384178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11384178/)
- A 2022 method automatically detects the ear tragus and C7 spinous process in a single lateral RGB image, using OpenPose plus image processing, so CVA can be computed. The authors evaluated it on a custom dataset and report "reliability ... particularly for head postures", but numerical agreement with manual CVA was not visible in the extract. — [BioMedInformatics 2022, doi 10.3390/biomedinformatics2020020](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedinformatics2020020)
- In a dentist-posture pilot (2D OpenPose), the "neck" keypoint served as a **C7 proxy** and the hip midpoint as a greater-trochanter proxy. The authors say the result should be read as a **"2D image-based trunk-inclination proxy"**. No formal error against a reference was visible. — [BDJ Open 2026, s41405-026-00487-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41405-026-00487-0)
- In a pilot validation of neck and trunk kinematics during a simulated surgery task (standing, multi-camera markerless vs reference), markerless methods showed **5.5–8.7° RMSE (ICC 0.31–0.70)**. The authors recommended IMUs over markerless methods for neck and trunk. — [Sensors 2022 / PMC9658075](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9658075/)
- A 2026 study compared single-camera 3D pose (MeTRAbs) with multi-camera Theia3D for gait, sit-to-stand and standing trunk flexion-extension. Joint-angle RMSE was **2.10–10.98°**, trunk flexion-extension and trunk inclination had the higher errors, and neck-base position RMSE was 5.30–6.84 cm. — [IEEE Access 2026, doi 10.1109/access.2026.3687207](https://doi.org/10.1109/access.2026.3687207); [PMC13229575](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13229575/)
- One markerless-validation paper warns that **small keypoint localization errors propagate into large angular errors for angles referenced to the vertical**. This applies directly to trunk inclination and CVA, which are both referenced to gravity or the horizontal. — [arXiv 2603.04864](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.04864)

**Occlusion and the far side**
- In single-camera markerless gait (PLoS ONE 2023), camera-side sagittal hip and knee angles were near marker-based error levels. **Occluded-side angles had about double the bias and limits of agreement**, and ankle LoA (about ±12°) were too wide for use. A single side camera therefore sees one side well and the other side poorly, so it cannot assess left–right asymmetry. — [PLoS ONE 2023 (DOAJ record)](https://doaj.org/article/46e7e953a98e4187a0bd28b5f4635451)
- In 2D video analysis of a fencing lunge vs 3D mocap, knee angles agreed (r = 0.93–0.99), but **hip angles correlated poorly (r = 0.31, bias about 11°)**. The authors recommend 3D for the hip/pelvis region. — [Front Sports Act Living 2024, fspor.2024.1335272](https://frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2024.1335272/text)
- Azure Kinect body tracking was tested with a seated subject behind a table (16 adults, frontal depth camera). Trunk-bending tasks stayed "equivalent" to the optoelectronic reference, but with a **consistent positive bias (trunk ROM underestimated)**. Median correlations were only moderate, and performance dropped sharply when camera distance increased. — [Sensors 2026, doi 10.3390/s26134098](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26134098)
- A separate Azure Kinect study found that **lower-limb occlusion worsened upper-limb accuracy**. This is indirect evidence that desk occlusion degrades whole-skeleton fitting. — [Heliyon 2023 / PMC10663858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10663858/)

**Sitting pelvis from a side camera**
- Kinovea (manual 2D video digitizing) for **pelvic** kinematics in standing and in sitting with 45° hip flexion: **13 asymptomatic participants**, inter- and intra-examiner ICC > 0.90, and good-to-excellent ICC vs Qualisys for **total pelvic ROM**. This was a ROM task, not a static seated tilt angle, and not in a desk chair. — [Sensors 2025, 25(1):250](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/1/250)

**Existing side-view seated examples (no validated angle output found)**
- A 2025 Scientific Reports study recorded seated posture with **two webcams (frontal and sagittal)** and used CVA for forward-head analysis, comparing therapist video-based RULA with an IMU system. The CVA agreement numbers were not visible in the extract. — [Sci Rep 2025, s41598-025-85159-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-85159-z)
- The Pose2Sim authors point to **Sports2D** for real-time analysis with **one camera, limited to motion in the sagittal or frontal plane**. No published Sports2D validation was found. — [Pose2Sim GitHub](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)

**iPhone as the side camera (Continuity Camera)**
- Continuity Camera webcam use requires **iPhone XR or later on iOS 16+** and a **Mac on macOS Ventura 13+**. Desk View requires iPhone 11 or later, excluding the iPhone 16e and SE models. Apple's requirement page states no Apple-silicon-only restriction, but the extracts seen did not explicitly confirm Intel Macs either. — [Apple: Continuity requirements](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT6555); [Apple: Use iPhone as a webcam](https://support.apple.com/102546)

### Inferences
- **What a side camera can measure well without markers:** gross trunk inclination (shoulder–hip line vs vertical), head-forward offset (ear keypoint vs shoulder keypoint, horizontally, normalized by a body length), and head pitch (ear–eye line). The ear–shoulder angle is a **CVA proxy, not CVA**. The shoulder keypoint sits on the acromion region, not C7, and moves when the shoulders protract. For someone with internally rotated or protracted shoulders, an ear–shoulder "CVA" can therefore look better or worse for reasons unrelated to the neck. This follows from the C7-absence point in [PMC11384178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11384178/) and the landmark-definition bias in the [MediaPipe deep-dive](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/398607117_A_Deep_Dive_into_MediaPipe_Pose_for_Postural_Assessment_A_Comparative_Investigation).
- **With 2 stickers (tragus + C7) in a side view**, the true CVA becomes computable by blob tracking in a fixed-position iPhone or webcam image. Accuracy should approach manual photogrammetry (Q5), *provided* the camera axis is perpendicular to the sagittal plane and level. Trunk rotation toward or away from the camera foreshortens horizontal distances, so CVA error grows with rotation; this is uncalibrated in the literature.
- **Not measurable from a seated side view:** thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis (chair back, clothing, arm occlusion); pelvic tilt (ASIS/PSIS hidden by the desk, armrests and clothing, with the hip keypoint about 5–10 cm from the pelvic landmarks); trunk rotation and left–right asymmetry (only one side visible; the far side is occluded). Rib flare is not observable through clothing.
- **Practical placement:** an iPhone on a stand at roughly shoulder height, 1.5–2.5 m to the side so the head-to-hip span fits, perpendicular to the sagittal plane. A desk-side placement often forces an oblique angle. Any obliquity of more than about 10–15° makes 2D sagittal angles a projection, which biases them in proportion to the cosine of the angle (an inference from projection geometry; no seated-specific study was found).
- **Continuous vs periodic:** a dedicated side webcam can run all day, though people move and rotate. An iPhone via Continuity Camera ties up the phone and is better suited to timed check-ins (for example, a 30-second capture a few times a day) than to all-day monitoring.

### Gaps
- No study was found that validates MediaPipe, Apple Vision or OpenPose **seated** CVA, neck flexion or trunk inclination from a side camera against mocap or photogrammetry, with ICC and Bland–Altman.
- No published numbers were found for the effect of desk or chair occlusion on side-view keypoint accuracy in sitting.
- No validation of Sports2D or of the 2022 automated tragus/C7 detector's agreement statistics was retrievable.
- Whether Continuity Camera works on the specific Intel iMac was not confirmed by Apple text in this session. Note that ARKit and Vision on the iPhone itself are an alternative that avoids Continuity entirely.

## 2. Depth cameras (Kinect v2, Azure Kinect, Orbbec Femto/Gemini, Intel/RealSense, iPhone TrueDepth/LiDAR + ARKit): seated trunk, spine and pelvis accuracy, spinal surface curvature, 2026 availability, macOS/Intel compatibility

### Takeaway
Depth sensing helps mainly in two ways: back-surface shape (kyphosis) when the bare or tight-clothed back faces the sensor, and a less ambiguous trunk/head 3D direction. Validated seated accuracy is modest. Kinect thoracic kyphosis correlated only r = 0.56 with Spinal Mouse, and lordosis showed no correlation. Azure Kinect trunk ROM behind a table was underestimated with moderate correlations. iPad LiDAR/ARKit raw joint-angle RMSE was 6.8–13.9° (gait). For this user's hardware, the decisive constraint is macOS support. Azure Kinect is discontinued. Orbbec's Femto Bolt is not supported on macOS by SDK v2, while the Gemini 330 series is (beta). librealsense on macOS needs sudo and lacks the Viewer and IMU. None ships a skeleton tracker that runs on macOS.

### Cited Findings
**Spinal surface curvature from depth or structured light**
- **Kinect vs Spinal Mouse (2023):** 24 healthy men, **standing**. Kinect ToF depth points in a marked back region gave summed segmental angles. Thoracic kyphosis means were 33.3 ± 9.1° (Kinect) vs 33.3 ± 7.4° (Spinal Mouse), with **r = 0.56 (moderate)**. **No significant correlation for lumbar lordosis.** The reference was Spinal Mouse, not radiography. — [J Orthop Surg Res 2023, 10.1186/s13018-023-03693-w](https://josr-online.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13018-023-03693-w)
- **Rasterstereography** (projected-light back-surface topography; the clinical analogue of a depth back scan), in Scheuermann's patients: thoracic kyphosis vs radiograph **r = 0.677 (T2–T12) and 0.704 (C7–max), ICC > 0.75**, with proportional bias against one radiographic measure. — [Eur Spine J 2024, s00586-024-08610-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-024-08610-1)
- Surface topography in scoliosis patients: thoracic kyphosis **r = 0.76, Bland–Altman mean difference 0.4°** vs radiograph. — [J Manipulative Physiol Ther 2021, S0161475421000786](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0161475421000786)
- Surface topography for lumbar curvature: **no correlation with radiograph (r = 0.32)**, so agreement could not be analysed. — [Spine Deformity 2022, s43390-022-00538-0](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s43390-022-00538-0)
- Video rasterstereography test–retest reliability was ICC > 0.90 for **pelvic tilt, kyphosis, lordosis and trunk inclination** (standing). This is reliability, not validity vs X-ray. — [Eur Spine J 2014, s00586-014-3664-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-014-3664-5)
- A 2026 prospective study validated an AI app that predicts scoliosis curve magnitude from **two smartphone videos of the bare back** (surface topography), compared against radiographs and with at-home vs in-clinic reliability. An earlier iPad + Structure Sensor torso scan separated scoliosis from controls with 95% accuracy. Both are standing, bare-back setups. — [PMC13043243](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13043243/)
- iPad-based Structure Sensor back and whole-body shape capture: intra- and inter-rater reliability in **16 young adults**; no radiographic validation. — [PMC10706066](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10706066/)

**Depth-camera skeletons in seated or upper-body tasks**
- **Azure Kinect, 2026:** 16 adults, 10 upper-body tasks under three conditions (optimal; **seated on a stool behind a table, 2.5 m from the camera**; increased distance). Metrics were nRMSE and Pearson r vs an optoelectronic system. Trunk-bending tasks were equivalent across conditions, but with a **consistent positive bias (underestimated trunk ROM)**. Median correlations were moderate in the optimal and seated conditions and deteriorated markedly at greater distance. — [Sensors 2026, 10.3390/s26134098](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26134098)
- **Azure Kinect vs Vicon** (standing reach): ICC > 0.9 for sternum and trunk tracking. Lower-limb occlusion worsened upper-limb accuracy. — [Heliyon 2023 / PMC10663858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10663858/)
- **Kinect v2, seated reaching:** moderate-to-excellent reliability for trunk compensation, hand ROM, movement time and velocity. This is reliability, not angle error. — [PMC9003545](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9003545/)
- A single Kinect (Schmitz et al.) agreed with an inclinometer to within **< 0.5°** for sagittal and frontal joint angles in a **static jig** test. This is a best-case, non-human condition. — [Univ. Kentucky record](https://scholars.uky.edu/en/publications/accuracy-and-repeatability-of-joint-angles-measured-using-a-singl/)
- SitPose (2024) used a Kinect depth sensor for real-time **sitting-posture classification** from 3D joints. It is a demo/classifier, not angle validation. — [arXiv 2412.12216](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.12216)

**iPhone/iPad ARKit body tracking (LiDAR/TrueDepth)**
- iPad Pro LiDAR + ARKit in treadmill gait (10 subjects): raw hip/knee flexion RMSE was **6.8–13.9°**. ARKit underestimated large knee flexion and full extension. A neural-network correction reduced errors to 2.5–3.8°. — [Gait & Posture 2022, S0966636222002533](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0966636222002533)
- ARKit vs Vicon during exercises: it tracks the **general progression** of movement but with **significant deviations from actual values**. Visibility-related spikes appeared, and performance depended on the motion. — [Applied Sciences 2022, app12104806](https://doi.org/10.3390/app12104806)
- `ARBodyTrackingConfiguration` tracks bodies with the **rear-facing camera** (iOS/iPadOS 13+). Developer-forum answers say body tracking is not available on the front TrueDepth camera and suggest Vision body pose instead. — [Apple ARKit docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arbodytrackingconfiguration); [Apple Developer Forums 93679](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/93679)
- **Apple Vision 3D body pose** (`VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest`, macOS 14 / iOS 17): 17 joints in metres relative to a root joint, one (most prominent) person. It reportedly uses AVDepthData when available, but this is a secondary blog claim, unverified against Apple text. **No published accuracy evaluation was found.** A developer forum notes it needs Neural-Engine hardware and is not supported in the Simulator. — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodypose3drequest); [WWDC23 notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-111241-explore-3d-body-pose-and-person-segmentation-in-vision/); [blakecrosley.com blog](https://blakecrosley.com/blog/vision-framework-built-in); [Visionaire (availability macOS 14)](https://github.com/alladinian/Visionaire); [Apple forum 743402](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/743402)

### Inferences
- **The best evidence for depth comes from back-surface topography (standing, bare back, sensor behind the person)**, not from skeleton tracking. Thoracic kyphosis is moderately-to-well estimated (r about 0.56–0.76). Lumbar lordosis is poor, and pelvic tilt has reliability data only. That is a **periodic check-in protocol** (stand with back to the camera, shirt off or tight top), not continuous seated monitoring: a chair backrest occludes the back while sitting.
- For continuous seated use, a depth camera in front of or beside the desk mainly adds a metric z-coordinate for the head and shoulders. That could measure head-forward distance in centimetres rather than a 2D proxy. No validation of that specific seated measure was found.
- **Skeleton tracking for Orbbec and Azure Kinect (Microsoft Body Tracking SDK) was historically Windows/Linux + GPU.** Combined with the Orbbec macOS notes above, a depth-camera skeleton on an Intel Mac would need custom code (for example, running 2D keypoints on the RGB stream and sampling aligned depth). This is an inference; no macOS body-tracking route was documented.
- **iPhone as the depth sensor:** ARKit body tracking runs on-device but only from the rear camera, so the phone must face the user, typically from the side, with its screen turned away. Accuracy at the trunk and spine level is unvalidated, and 2022 evidence shows 7–14° raw joint-angle errors. Apple Vision 3D body pose (17 joints, including spine/root) is the only Apple API with a "spine" point, but it is unvalidated.
- Cheapest macOS-plausible depth hardware: Orbbec Gemini 335 (about $264, macOS beta) or RealSense D405/D435i (about $325–399, sudo, no Viewer). An iPhone with LiDAR or TrueDepth that the user already owns costs nothing extra.

### Gaps
- No depth-camera study was found that validates **seated** spinal curvature or **seated pelvic tilt** against radiography or mocap.
- No RealSense-specific validation for spinal curvature was found.
- No accuracy data was found for Apple's `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest` or for current-iOS ARKit body tracking. The studies found date from 2022.
- Whether Orbbec SDK v2's latest (2026) release added Femto Bolt macOS support, and whether `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest` runs on an Intel iMac without a Neural Engine, were both unresolved.
- Kinect v2's discontinuation date was not re-verified this session; treat it as long discontinued and available only second-hand.

## 3. Multi-camera markerless 3D (OpenCap, Pose2Sim, FreeMoCap, Theia3D): trunk/pelvis accuracy vs marker mocap, seated/office suitability, setup effort, local execution

### Takeaway
Multi-camera markerless systems reach about 3–5° mean joint-angle error for lower-limb sagittal motion. Trunk and pelvis remain their weak spot, and none was validated for seated desk posture. Reported values: pelvic tilt RMSE about 3.6–5.1° in pooled OpenCap studies, trunk RMSE 2.8–19° (worst transverse), and neck ICC only 0.56–0.66. OpenCap needs two iPhones plus cloud processing, or an NVIDIA CUDA GPU for local runs. Pose2Sim runs locally on CPU but offline only, with synchronized and calibrated cameras. Theia3D costs tens of thousands of dollars and needs 8 cameras. For a single person at a desk this is a periodic, lab-style check, not a continuous monitor.

### Cited Findings
- **OpenCap (Uhlrich et al., PLOS Comput Biol 2023):** two iPhones vs marker mocap and force plates in **10 healthy adults** (walking, squatting, sit-to-stand, drop jumps). Joint angles had **MAE 4.5°**, GRF 6.2% body weight and joint moments 1.2% BW·ht. Marker position error was 38 mm at the pelvis and 39 mm at the upper extremity, vs 27 mm for the lower extremity. — [PLOS Comput Biol](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1011462); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.07.499061v1.full)
- In the same paper, with raw video keypoints only, errors of **12.1–39.2° at lumbar extension, pelvic tilt and hip flexion** were attributed to the limited keypoint marker set. OpenCap's "marker augmentation" (predicting anatomical markers) improved those DOFs by 4.9–32.6°. Per-DOF final MAEs are in Table 1, which was not visible. — [bioRxiv full text](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.07.07.499061v1.full); [PLOS version](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pcbi.1011462)
- OpenCap developers on SimTK: lumbar coordinates were among the higher-error DOFs. Lumbar extension is a trunk-relative-to-pelvis angle, so trunk inclination requires combining it with pelvic tilt. — [SimTK forum](https://simtk.org/plugins/phpBB/viewtopic.php?t=16394)
- 2026 scoping review of OpenCap validations (few studies, n = 2–4 per variable): **pelvic tilt RMSE about 3.6–5.1°; trunk RMSE about 2.8–19°**, with the largest errors in the transverse plane (rotation). — [Front Digit Health 2026, fdgth.2026.1882536](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1882536/full)
- OpenCap trunk and lower-limb validity in squat, hop, jump and side-step: the squat had the lowest RMSE, a mean of **7.0° (range 2.9–13.6°)**, including trunk angles. — [J Sports Sci 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2024.2415233)
- OpenCap in return-to-sport tasks: RMSD < 6° **except pelvic tilt**, hip flexion, ankle inversion, foot progression and transverse rotations. — [J Biomech 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021929024002781)
- **OpenCap processing:** cloud processing at app.opencap.ai is the default. Local processing requires at least a **CUDA GPU with 4 GB** (8 GB for cloud-equivalent OpenPose settings, 24 GB for high-resolution), on Windows 10 or Ubuntu. An older SimTK reply (about 2 years old) said the full pipeline could not be reproduced locally. — [opencap-core GitHub](https://github.com/opencap-org/opencap-core); [DeepWiki summary](https://deepwiki.com/opencap-org/opencap-core); [SimTK forum](https://simtk.org/plugins/phpBB/viewtopic.php?f=2385&t=19035)
- **OpenCap Monocular (preprint, Mar 2026):** a single smartphone video, WHAM-based pose refined by optimization onto a constrained skeleton. Rotational DOFs had **MAE about 4.8°** and pelvis translation 3.4 cm vs mocap, for **walking, squatting and sit-to-stand**. It is free and open-source, but not peer-reviewed in the sources seen, and per-joint tables (trunk/pelvis) were not visible. — [arXiv 2603.24733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733); [project page](https://utahmobl.github.io/OpenCap-monocular-project-page/)
- **Pose2Sim accuracy (Sensors 2022):** lower-body walking, running and cycling showed mean errors of about **3–4°** vs markers. On the same data, Theia3D RMSE ranged 2.6–13.2°, on average 1.5° higher than Pose2Sim. — [Sensors 2022, 22(7):2712](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/7/2712); [JOSS paper](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04362.pdf)
- **Pose2Sim practicalities:** real-time 3D is on the to-do list, not supported (Sports2D is suggested for real-time single-camera 2D). Calibration requires **synchronized cameras**, and keypoint-based extrinsic calibration needs the subject visible in at least 2 cameras. A `device: CPU` option exists, and `multi_person = false` is much faster. — [Pose2Sim GitHub](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- RTMPose (the default 2D model in Pose2Sim) is reported at > 430 FPS on CPU, GPU or mobile for the model itself, not the full pipeline. — [RTMPose summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2303.07399)
- **Camera geometry (2026):** a multi-view markerless study found **about 45° separation between camera pairs** optimal: static ICC 0.851, MAE 9.28°; dynamic ICC 0.823, MAE 12.92°. Hips, knees and shoulders had ICC 0.73–0.92, but the **neck only 0.56–0.66**. — [BMC Res Notes 2026, s13104-026-07886-4](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13104-026-07886-4)
- **Theia3D:** no public list price. Academic and non-profit buyers get a lifetime licence, while commercial buyers pay annually. The vendor says a **minimum of 8 video cameras** for most applications and a gaming desktop of about **$5,000–7,000**, and that it is intended for research, sports and education only. One procurement listing has an AI-estimated $50k–150k for two licences (unverified). — [Theia blog: Budgeting](https://www.theiamarkerless.com/blog/budgeting-for-a-markerless-system); [Theia FAQ](https://www.theiamarkerless.com/faq); [HigherGov listing](https://www.highergov.com/sl/contract-opportunity/sc-theia3d-markerless-motion-capture-system-61793032/)
- **FreeMoCap:** no peer-reviewed accuracy validation was found. A validation repository (Qualisys comparison tooling) exists without published results. — [freemocap_validation GitHub](https://github.com/aaroncherian/freemocap_validation)

### Inferences
- **Seated, at a desk:** all of these systems fit a full-body skeleton. A desk hides the hips and legs from front cameras, and the chair hides the back, so pelvis fitting (already the weakest DOF while standing) will be worse still. No seated-desk validation exists. **Trunk rotation** (transverse plane) is where errors reach about 19°.
- A **two-iPhone OpenCap session** (calibration checkerboard, two phones at about 30–45° on either side of the front, sitting on a stool without a desk) is a credible **periodic** protocol for trunk and pelvis *trends* in standing or stool-sitting, plus sit-to-stand. It is cloud-processed, so videos leave the device. That conflicts with the privacy preference unless the user accepts it for occasional sessions, or runs local processing on a CUDA machine, which an Intel iMac with an AMD GPU is not.
- **Pose2Sim with two webcams** (for example, the iMac camera plus a USB webcam or Continuity iPhone) can run entirely locally on CPU but **offline** (record, then process). It needs synchronization and calibration work and Python tooling outside the Swift app. It suits occasional check-ins, not a menu-bar live monitor.
- For one person's clinical sharing, multi-camera markerless systems do not offer a validated spine-curvature or pelvic-tilt number. They give skeleton-segment angles with about 4–6° (pelvis) to 19° (rotation) uncertainty.

### Gaps
- No validation was found of any multi-camera markerless system for **seated office posture** (neck flexion, CVA, thoracic angle, pelvic tilt in a desk chair).
- Exact OpenCap Table 1 per-DOF values (pelvic tilt, pelvic list, lumbar extension, bending, rotation) were not retrievable.
- Current (2026) state of local and offline OpenCap processing, and any macOS or Apple-silicon support, was not confirmed.
- No FreeMoCap trunk or pelvis accuracy data was found.

## 4. Monocular 3D body-model/mesh estimation (SMPL-based 4DHumans/HMR 2.0, WHAM, TokenHMR, CameraHMR; Apple 3D body APIs): validation for seated spine/pelvis; can it recover forward head or pelvic tilt from a frontal or oblique view?

### Takeaway
No validation was found of SMPL-based monocular mesh methods for seated spine, pelvis, CVA or forward-head angles. The biomechanics literature finds that raw SMPL/HMR outputs have anatomically inaccurate joint centers and orientations. Monocular pipelines reach only about 5–6° even for sagittal lower-limb angles, with out-of-plane angles rarely validated. The best monocular result, OpenCap Monocular at about 4.8° MAE, comes from heavy biomechanical post-processing for walking, squat and sit-to-stand, not desk sitting. Recovering pelvic tilt or CVA from a frontal webcam is not supported by evidence. These models output a plausible average-body pose, not a measurement.

### Cited Findings
- A 2026 scoping review of video-based markerless capture for clinical biomechanics found most monocular pipelines report raw joint angles without biomechanical refinement. **Sagittal lower-limb agreement clustered around 5–6°**, "generally short of clinical acceptability", and **out-of-plane kinematics were rarely validated**. — [arXiv 2609.18667](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.18667)
- A benchmark of **11 monocular pose estimators** against marker-based ground truth (2.2M frames) reportedly found **none met a 5° joint-angle error threshold**. This is a secondhand claim, and the primary paper was not identified. — [search extract via BioPose/related](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07800)
- BioPose (2025): state-of-the-art mesh-recovery outputs "lack biomechanically defined joint angles". HMR 2.0's skeleton is "physically inaccurate". The proposed fix treats SMPL vertices as virtual markers and regresses onto a biomechanical skeleton. — [BioPose, arXiv 2501.07800](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.07800)
- MonoMSK (2025): single-image SMPL regressors "rely on oversimplified skeletal structures with anatomically inaccurate joint positions and bone orientations, fundamentally limiting their biomechanical fidelity". It uses virtual markers with anatomy-constrained alignment. — [MonoMSK, arXiv 2511.19326](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19326)
- In pathological (simulated) gait, monocular **CameraHMR** had considerably higher errors than OpenCap. The authors partly blame the SMPL model's fixed ankle-joint position. — [ResearchGate 396030456](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/396030456_Validity_and_reliability_of_monocular_3D_markerless_gait_analysis_in_simulated_pathological_gait_A_comparative_study_with_OpenCap)
- **OpenCap Monocular** builds on WHAM plus optimization: 4.8° rotational MAE for walking, squat and sit-to-stand (preprint). — [arXiv 2603.24733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24733)
- A 2025 mobile tool reconstructs SMPL from **multiple views** (contour consistency) for standing posture. It reports > 90% accuracy *in classifying* abnormalities such as uneven shoulders and forward head posture, which is classification, not angle agreement. — [Med Biol Eng Comput 2025, s11517-025-03493-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11517-025-03493-w)
- Forward-head detection from 3D pose + GCN reached 78.27% classification accuracy, and the authors note pose models lack C7/CVA. — [PMC11384178](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11384178/)
- PosturePose proposes a bone-orientation error metric (including spine and pelvis segments) for monocular 3D pose, but not for sitting and not against clinical angles. — [Sensors 2023, PMC10747870](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10747870/)
- Apple's `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest` (macOS 14+) returns a 17-joint skeleton in metres for one person, with no published accuracy evaluation. — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vndetecthumanbodypose3drequest); [WWDC23 notes](https://wwdcnotes.com/documentation/wwdc23-111241-explore-3d-body-pose-and-person-segmentation-in-vision/)

### Inferences
- **Frontal view:** forward head and pelvic tilt are both mainly *depth-axis* (anterior–posterior) quantities. A monocular model infers depth from learned priors (typical human poses and SMPL shape space), so individual deviations such as an anteriorly tilted pelvis under clothing, or 2–3 cm of extra head protrusion, tend to be regressed toward the training-set mean. The output would be smooth and plausible but biased toward "normal". This follows from the anatomical-inaccuracy critiques above; no direct test exists.
- **SMPL has no separate spine-curvature parameters beyond 3 spine joints** (spine1–3 in standard SMPL; this is from model knowledge, not a cited source). Thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis cannot be represented with clinical fidelity. Pelvic tilt is the root orientation, which in sitting is confounded with the chair and thigh pose.
- **Oblique view (about 30–45°)** gives the model some sagittal information. It is the best single-camera compromise for the 3D mesh approach, but still unvalidated for seated posture.
- Compute: WHAM, HMR 2.0, TokenHMR and CameraHMR are PyTorch ViT-scale models designed for GPUs. On an Intel iMac CPU they would be far below real-time (an inference; no benchmark found), so they fit only offline analysis of short clips. Apple's 3D Vision request is the only native, on-device option, and it lacks validation.
- **Bottom line for the app:** use mesh or 3D-pose outputs at most as *exploratory, non-clinical* trend signals with explicit "unvalidated" labelling, never as numbers to share with a clinician.

### Gaps
- No study was found that compares any SMPL-family method (HMR 2.0/4DHumans, WHAM, TokenHMR, CameraHMR) with mocap or radiography for **trunk flexion, thoracic angle, pelvic tilt or CVA in sitting**.
- No CPU benchmarks (Intel x86) for these models were found.
- No accuracy data was found for Apple's 3D body pose on seated users.

## 5. Marker-assisted approaches (stickers on tragus, C7, acromion, ASIS/PSIS) with a phone camera for periodic self-assessment

### Takeaway
Sticker-based photogrammetry is the best-validated low-cost camera method for **CVA**. Pooled across 29 studies and 1,910 participants, test-retest ICC is 0.90 and inter-rater ICC 0.89, with good-to-excellent but heterogeneous validity, and MDC about 5° in one app study. It is much weaker for **thoracic and lumbar curvature** (photogrammetry vs radiographic Cobb: weak thoracic, moderate lumbar, "validation not confirmed") and for **pelvic tilt**. Pelvic landmarks are hard to palpate and re-mark consistently, and only a 13-person Kinovea study covers sitting. Self-application of the C7 or PSIS stickers by a lone user is an additional unvalidated error source.

### Cited Findings
- **2025 systematic review and meta-analysis** of photogrammetry smartphone apps for spinal posture: **29 studies, 1,910 participants**. Pooled CVA ICC was 0.904 (test-retest) and 0.889 (inter-rater). CVA validity was good to excellent but heterogeneous (I² = 84.82%). Hip tilt, head tilt and acromion alignment showed moderate-to-excellent reliability. — [Sci Rep 2025, s41598-025-32708-1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-32708-1)
- **CVA app validation (2020):** 44 subjects with **tragus and C7 markers** vs photogrammetry. Criterion validity was > 0.82, intra-rater ICC 0.88 and inter-rater ICC 0.83–0.89, with sensitivity 94.4% and specificity 84.6% for FHP. **MDC was about 5–5.5°.** — [PMC7559098](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7559098/)
- **Computer-vision head-posture app** using 2D markers at C7 and tragus: inter-rater ICC 0.91, concurrent validity r = 0.94. — [Applied Sciences 2023, 13(6):3910](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/13/6/3910)
- A further smartphone CVA app study covered different age groups and positions (clinimetric properties); figures were not visible. — [PMC10558335](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10558335/)
- **PostureScreen Mobile:** a systematic review of non-radiographic FHP methods rates it excellent for intra-rater reliability and high for validity, but with **moderate** evidence. Other PostureScreen studies report inter-rater ICCs of **0.26–0.93 across 11 variables** and **−0.12 to 0.96** in another study. The first validity study was described as having uncertain results. — [PMC9354067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9354067/); [ResearchGate 322729832](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322729832_Rater_reliability_and_construct_validity_of_a_mobile_application_for_posture_analysis); [Semantic Scholar PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/d83e/c023fa58662a07b6f0cb576a778738d3e46b.pdf)
- **Photogrammetry vs radiographic Cobb angles (2025):** 45 volunteers, external markers at C7, T12 and L5. Correlations were **moderate for lumbar and weak for thoracic** angles. Reliability was high, but the authors state **validation was not confirmed** and recommend photogrammetry only as a complementary tool. — [Measurement 2025, S0263224125026661](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0263224125026661)
- **Smartphone inclinometer** (phone placed on the back, not a camera) for standing thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis: ICC 0.92 and 0.90, but **n = 12**. — [PMC11751372](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11751372/)
- A smartphone inclinometer method for thoracolumbar kyphosis (osteoporotic fracture patients) vs PACS radiographic measurement: mean difference **0.4°, LoA −2.4 to 3.2°**. The extract implies the comparison was on radiographic images, so it is not evidence for skin-surface measurement. — [Sci Rep 2022, s41598-022-12690-8](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-12690-8)
- **Pelvic landmarks:** the literature suggests **relatively low reliability for palpating ASIS and PSIS** in healthy adults. The simple ASIS-vs-PSIS height classification has no known reliability or validity testing, and **inclinometers may be better than visual or palpation methods** for pelvic tilt. — [IJSPT: Clinical Measures of Pelvic Tilt](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/27978-clinical-measures-of-pelvic-tilt-in-physical-therapy)
- Repositioning markers between sessions is a common reliability problem, and **re-marking ASIS/PSIS centres is "particularly problematic"**. This is from a photographic-posture study; attribution from the search extract is uncertain. — [BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2020 (Global Postural System)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12891-020-03617-3)
- **Kinovea** sitting pelvic ROM vs Qualisys: n = 13, ICC > 0.90, sitting at 45° hip flexion. — [Sensors 2025, 25(1):250](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/1/250)
- Automated tragus/C7 detection in RGB images is possible (OpenPose + image processing), but agreement statistics were not visible. — [BioMedInformatics 2022](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomedinformatics2020020)

### Inferences
- **The most defensible "precise" camera measurement for this user's private records is a standardized side photo or short clip with stickers on tragus and C7** (and optionally acromion, for a shoulder-forward angle). Take it at the same chair, camera height and distance, with the phone levelled, several times per day or week. Report CVA with an uncertainty band of about ±5° (MDC), and treat changes smaller than that as noise.
- **Self-placement:** C7 can be located by the user (most prominent spinous process at the neck base when flexing), but consistency is unvalidated for self-application. A fixed tragus sticker is easy. ASIS is self-palpable, but PSIS is hard to self-locate and hidden by the chair when seated. **Seated pelvic tilt by stickers is therefore impractical for a lone user at a desk.** A standing side photo with ASIS/PSIS stickers, ideally placed once by a clinician and photographed for reference, is the more realistic periodic protocol, with the reliability caveats above.
- **Asymmetry** (user's left buttock back, one side of the back touching the backrest): a **posterior photo** (camera behind, standing) with stickers on both acromia, both PSIS and the inferior scapular angles gives frontal-plane obliquity and asymmetry measures (meta-analysis: hip tilt and acromion alignment had moderate-to-excellent reliability). Seated transverse pelvic rotation, as when one buttock sits further back, is best captured from **above or behind** the chair, not from the desk camera. No validated camera protocol for seated pelvic rotation was found.
- The app could automate sticker detection (bright, coloured, round dots), which is simpler and more robust than learned keypoints, and could store each measurement with the photo, for clinician review, on-device.

### Gaps
- No validation was found of **self-applied** markers (without an examiner) for CVA or pelvic landmarks.
- No validated photogrammetry protocol was found for **shoulder protraction or internal rotation** (for example, forward-shoulder angle from acromion vs C7), for **rib flare**, or for **seated pelvic rotation**. Targeted searches were not done within the call budget.
- Exact agreement statistics (Bland–Altman) for marker-based CVA vs radiographic cervical measures were not retrieved.

## 6. Which of these can run on an Intel Mac or iPhone without cloud, and at what frame rate/CPU cost

### Takeaway
Fully local, real-time options on this hardware are: Apple Vision 2D body pose and face landmarks (already in use; works on Intel); MediaPipe Pose (about 25–38 ms/frame on a 2017 MacBook Pro in Google's legacy table); colour-sticker blob tracking (negligible CPU); and ARKit or Vision on the iPhone itself. Apple Vision 3D body pose needs macOS 14, and whether it runs on an Intel iMac is unclear. Depth cameras on macOS are limited to Orbbec Gemini 330 (beta SDK) or RealSense (sudo, no Viewer), with no body tracking. OpenCap needs cloud or an NVIDIA GPU. Pose2Sim is local CPU but offline. SMPL mesh models are GPU-class and far from real-time on an Intel CPU.

### Cited Findings
- MediaPipe legacy Pose latency table (BlazePose GHUM Lite/Full/Heavy): **25 / 27 / 38 ms per frame on a 2017 MacBook Pro** and 20 / 25 / 53 ms on a Pixel 3 with TFLite GPU. It is unclear whether the MacBook figures are CPU-only. Accuracy increases and speed falls from Lite to Heavy. — [MediaPipe pose docs (GitHub)](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/blob/master/docs/solutions/pose.md); [TensorFlow blog 2021](https://blog.tensorflow.org/2021/05/high-fidelity-pose-tracking-with-mediapipe-blazepose-and-tfjs.html)
- Apple Vision 2D human body pose (`DetectHumanBodyPoseRequest` / `VNDetectHumanBodyPoseRequest`) is an on-device Vision API. — [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/detecthumanbodyposerequest)
- `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest`: macOS 14 / iOS 17+. A forum answer says it needs Neural-Engine hardware but may work on some Intel Macs. — [Visionaire](https://github.com/alladinian/Visionaire); [Apple forum 743402](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/743402)
- ARKit body tracking uses the iPhone/iPad rear camera, processed on-device. — [Apple ARKit docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arbodytrackingconfiguration)
- Continuity Camera: macOS 13+, iPhone XR+ on iOS 16+. — [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-us/HT6555)
- OpenCap: cloud (app.opencap.ai) or local with a CUDA GPU of at least 4 GB. — [opencap-core](https://github.com/opencap-org/opencap-core)
- Pose2Sim: CPU device option; no real-time 3D. — [Pose2Sim GitHub](https://github.com/perfanalytics/pose2sim)
- RTMPose: > 430 FPS at the model level across CPU, GPU and mobile (paper claim; hardware unspecified in the extract). — [RTMPose summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2303.07399)
- Orbbec SDK v2 macOS beta (Intel 10.15+). Supported: Gemini 330 series, Femto Mega, Gemini 2/2L, Astra 2. **Femto Bolt not supported.** — [OrbbecSDK_v2 releases](https://github.com/orbbec/OrbbecSDK_v2/releases)
- librealsense macOS: sudo required, Viewer unsupported, IMU disabled; Homebrew Intel bottles for Ventura and Sonoma. — [RealSense doc](https://dev.realsenseai.com/?p=40689); [Homebrew](https://formulae.brew.sh/formula/librealsense)
- Theia3D: Windows gaming desktop at about $5–7k plus 8 cameras. — [Theia blog](https://www.theiamarkerless.com/blog/budgeting-for-a-markerless-system)

### Inferences

- **Practical recommendation pattern (inference, consistent with the no-diagnosis principle):**
  1. **Continuous tier (all day):** keep the frontal Vision tier for *relative* signals (head height or distance changes from the user's own baseline, frontal tilt), and label them as relative, not clinical angles.
  2. **Add a side camera** (cheap USB webcam on a stand, or an iPhone during check-ins) running Apple Vision 2D pose to log a sagittal ear–shoulder–hip proxy and trunk inclination, again relative to personal baseline. The Vision requests are already used in the app, so no new dependency is needed.
  3. **Periodic "precision" tier (weekly, or before a clinic visit):** a guided capture with stickers on tragus, C7 and acromion (side view: CVA, shoulder-forward proxy), plus a standing posterior photo with acromion and PSIS stickers (frontal-plane asymmetry). Optionally, a standing bare-back depth scan with an iPhone (TrueDepth/LiDAR) for a kyphosis trend. Store the photos and numbers locally with the uncertainty (±MDC) for sharing with a clinician.
  4. **Skip for this use case:** Theia3D (cost), Azure Kinect (discontinued), Femto Bolt (no macOS), SMPL mesh models as clinical numbers (unvalidated, GPU), and OpenCap (cloud, unless the user explicitly accepts occasional uploads).
- **CPU budget on the Intel iMac:** sampling Apple Vision 2D pose at 0.2–1 Hz (rather than 30 fps) should keep CPU load in the low single-digit percent range. This is an inference from the 25–38 ms per-frame figures; benchmark it on the actual machine.

### Gaps
- No measured x86-CPU fps for current MediaPipe Pose Landmarker `.task` models or for Apple Vision body-pose requests on Intel Macs was found. Benchmark locally.
- Whether `VNDetectHumanBodyPose3DRequest` runs on the user's Intel iMac (no Neural Engine) is unresolved.
- The exact Intel iMac model and year (CPU and GPU) are unknown, which affects all compute estimates.
- AirPods head-motion (IMU) sensing is outside the camera/vision scope of these notes and was not researched here.
