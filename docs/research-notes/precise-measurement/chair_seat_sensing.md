## Q1. What do seat pressure-mapping studies show about measuring seated pelvic posture and asymmetry (CoP vs pelvic tilt/obliquity by mocap or radiography; ischial pressure asymmetry)?

### Takeaway
Seat centre of pressure (CoP) clearly moves with pelvic tilt. The best direct evidence is n=28 healthy adults with motion capture, where pelvic tilt was the segment most consistently associated with CoP displacement in every lean direction. Sacral force tracks posterior pelvic tilt at r≈0.96. No study found validates seat CoP or ischial-pressure asymmetry as a calibrated estimate of a radiographic pelvic angle (degrees of tilt, obliquity or rotation). The only pressure-plus-radiograph work is a 30-person Korean smart-chair intervention study without a control group, plus a 20-person pilot (NCT07062510) that has no published results. Seat pressure is therefore a sensitive, repeatable *relative* index (change over time, left/right ratio) but not a validated *pelvic-angle* measurement.

### Cited Findings
**CoP vs pelvic tilt (motion capture / force)**
- Liu et al., *Frontiers in Rehabilitation Sciences* (2026): 28 healthy volunteers each performed seated trunk leans twice in the left, right, forward and backward directions. Researchers recorded CoP displacement together with head, trunk and pelvic-tilt angle changes, plus hip angle for forward-backward leans, and analysed them with mixed-effects models. Between-trial ICC was 0.84–0.95. Pelvic-tilt angle change was significantly associated with CoP displacement in both left-right and forward-backward leaning (p<0.001). Head, trunk and hip angles were also associated with CoP in forward-backward leaning, but head and trunk showed no comparable association in left-right leaning. The authors conclude that pelvic shifts are central to sitting balance. — [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/rehabilitation-sciences/articles/10.3389/fresc.2026.1837819/full); [PubMed 42358806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42358806/); [PMC13290909](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13290909/)
- Kemmoku, Furumachi & Shimamura, *Prosthetics and Orthotics International* (2013) used a custom force-plate apparatus with separate sacrococcygeal and ischial plates. The correlation between posterior pelvic tilt and vertical force on the sacrococcygeal plate was 0.96, and sacrococcygeal force increased with pelvic tilt in all subjects. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0309364612465429)
- NTU pilot: 6 healthy men in an adjustable wheelchair, with motion analysis of the pelvis and a pressure plate, across four chair tilt angles. The pelvis tilted posteriorly as the backrest tilted back, maximum pressure fell, and the peak-pressure point moved toward the back of the seat. — [NTU report PDF](https://scholars.lib.ntu.edu.tw/bitstream/123456789/200581/1/912314B002393.pdf)
- PolyU thesis (2014): a pressure sheet on the seat pan was used across nine sitting postures. 34 of 36 posture pairs (94.4%) differed significantly in AP/ML CoP and contact area. Pelvic angle was not measured directly. — [PolyU thesis record](https://theses.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/200/7808)
- Hobson & Tooms (1992), as tabulated by SCIRE, studied a mixed SCI and able-bodied group. From neutral, 30° and 50° forward trunk flexion shifted the maximum-pressure location 2.4 cm and 2.7 cm, which the table calls a "posterior" shift. A 120° backrest recline gave the largest posterior shift, 6 cm. **(secondary; check full text: the snippet's "posterior" shift under forward flexion looks counter-intuitive)** — [SCIRE Table 18 PDF](https://scireproject.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/Table-18.-Effects-of-Seating-Equipment-Set-up-on-Posture-and-Postural-Alignment.pdf)
- A 2026 cross-sectional study of older adults in long-term care derived "posterior pelvic tilt" and "pelvic obliquity" indices from pressure-map AP CoP displacement and ischial-tuberosity pressure. These indices are CoP geometry, not independently measured angles, and the study links slumped sitting with chronic LBP. — [ScienceDirect S0197457226003903](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0197457226003903)
- Children with cerebral palsy: CoP position relative to the ischial tuberosities could identify adverse postures involving AP pelvic tilt or pelvic obliquity. **(secondary)** — [Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 2011](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.3109/17483107.2010.512969)

**Ischial / buttock pressure asymmetry and radiographic measures**
- Sohn et al., *Medicine* (2022), Korea. 30 participants with spinopelvic malalignment (inclusion: shoulder or iliac-crest height difference >5 mm) used an IoT smart chair with a height-adjustable seat plate. Left-right buttock pressure discrepancy fell from 36.4±32.3 (initial) to 15.7±20.3 (balanced) to 12.7±10.9 (after 1 h balanced), with p=.003 and .008 (ANOVA). The excerpts disagree on the unit, reporting both "Ω" and "mm". Coronal radiographic parameters were shoulder height difference, iliac-crest height difference, leg-length discrepancy, pelvic oblique angle and coronal imbalance. In standing radiographs after chair use, shoulder height difference, iliac-crest height difference and LLD improved significantly. The study has no control group. **(check full text)** — [PubMed 35119044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35119044); [LWW full text](https://journals.lww.com/md-journal/fulltext/2022/02040/the_radiographic_assessments_of_spino_pelvic.47.aspx)
- NCT07062510, "The Effects of Balanced Seating Pressure on Pelvic Asymmetry: A Pilot Study", Chang Gung Memorial Hospital. Planned n=20, aged 20–50. It compares a DaSuit® cushion with a placebo cushion, using a textile pressure mat on the cushion (seated on a 40×40×40 box) and frontal full-spine radiographs. The radiographic asymmetry criterion is a pelvic height discrepancy ≥0.5 cm. ClinicalTrials.gov lists the study as COMPLETED, with primary completion on 2026-03-27, while mirror sites still show "recruiting". No results or publication were found. — [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07062510); [trial mirror](https://decentrialz.com/clinical-trials/effects-balanced-seating-pressure-pelvic-asymmetry-07062510)
- Hwang et al., *Physical Therapy Korea* 21(1):37–46 (2014): 10 people with unilateral LBP and 10 without typed for 30 minutes on a pressure mat. In the LBP group, left-right pressure asymmetry at the end was significantly greater than at the start (p<.05). Pelvic tilt was measured after the session, but the excerpt cut off before that result. — [KoreaScience PDF](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201416760764215.pdf); [KoreaScholar](https://db.koreascholar.com/Article/Detail/268603)
- Pinupong et al. (Thai Journal of Physical Therapy / TJE): 26 adults with asymmetric seated weight-bearing used a real-time weight-distribution sensor for 15 minutes of biofeedback, against a control group that watched a video. The left-to-right buttock ratio went from 1.16±0.06 to 1.09±0.09 (p=0.049) in the experimental group. — [TJE](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/TJE/article/view/264637)
- Brno thesis, 90 manual-wheelchair users with SCI: pelvic obliquity was common. 14 patients (15.5%) seated with obliquity had *higher* pressure under the ischial tuberosity on the side *opposite* the obliquity. — [is.muni.cz thesis](https://is.muni.cz/th/g6yex/)
- Ischial tuberosities normally appear as two pressure peaks. One published pipeline finds the "ischial footprint" as a pair of close peaks and chooses between candidate pairs by centroid geometry. — [arXiv 1811.09107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.09107)
- Review-level limit: "pressure sensors alone are incapable of measuring the spinal trunk angle." — [Smart Sensing Chairs review (preprint)](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202403.1695); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380391638_Smart_Sensing_Chairs_for_Sitting_Posture_Detection_Classification_and_Monitoring_A_Comprehensive_Review)

### Inferences
- For this user, the defensible seat-pressure outputs are relative and longitudinal:
  - left/right load ratio, which is like Pinupong's L/R buttock ratio
  - mediolateral (ML) and anteroposterior (AP) CoP, measured against the user's own baseline
  - left and right ischial-peak locations, from paired-peak detection
  - the AP offset between the left and right ischial peaks, a candidate proxy for "the left buttock sits further back"

  None of these maps onto a radiographic angle, so the app should present them in raw units (%, mm) without "pelvic angle" labels.
- Liu 2026 shows that voluntary lean moves CoP through pelvic tilt. A shift in ML CoP is therefore ambiguous: it could reflect a lean or a structural or habitual pelvic asymmetry. The Brno finding that the heavier side can be opposite to the obliquity shows that the *direction* of an asymmetry cannot be read off pressure alone. This supports the no-direction / no-diagnosis principle.
- Anterior pelvic tilt should, mechanically, move load forward toward the thighs and away from the sacrum, the mirror image of Kemmoku's posterior-tilt finding. No study found validates seat pressure as a measure of *anterior* tilt specifically.
- Once the pending X-ray and leg-length measurement are available, a self-recorded seat-pressure baseline taken on the same day gives the clinician a paired reference. That is the most useful clinical role for these data.

### Gaps
- No study found that regresses seat CoP or ischial asymmetry against *radiographic* pelvic tilt, obliquity or transverse rotation (degrees), or that reports agreement statistics (Bland–Altman, RMSE) for that relationship.
- No study found that validates ischial-peak AP offset as a measure of transverse pelvic rotation.
- Not retrieved: Liu 2026 regression slopes (mm of CoP per degree of tilt) and whether pelvic obliquity was measured during left-right leans; the sitting-radiograph results and full limitations of Sohn 2022; the outcomes of NCT07062510.

## Q2. Smart-chair / smart-cushion research: accuracy for classifying postures (upright, slumped, lean L/R, crossed legs), personal vs generic models, long-term office studies

### Takeaway
Posture *classification* is mature in the lab:
- 80–98% accuracy for 6–19 prescribed postures with 4 to 2×1024 sensors
- typically 78–91% on unseen people
- about 92–98% when the model is trained on the same person

Reported gaps between personal and generic models are about 13–17 points (eCushion 92%→79%; Tan 96%→79%). Free-living validation is scarce: a 2021 systematic review found only 2 of 14 studies outside the lab. Validated office field data exist mainly for *sitting time*, not posture. For a single user, a personal model is the evidence-supported choice.

### Cited Findings
**Chair systems with force or pressure sensors**
- Zemp et al., *BioMed Research International* (2016), ETH Zurich. An office chair instrumented with force and acceleration sensors (16 force-sensor values plus backrest angle as inputs) was tested with 41 subjects in 7 prescribed positions, giving 1,148 samples. Five ML methods were compared with leave-one-out cross-validation. Random Forest was best, with mean accuracy of 90.9% for subjects the model had not seen and 81–98% per position. — [PMC5102712](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5102712/); [ETH Research Collection](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/3eaa4f41-f88c-4285-b594-c2a312f42776)
- Tan et al., Purdue "sensing chair" (2000/2001): pressure sensors in the seat and backrest, tested on 30 people. Overall accuracy was 96% for classifying slouching, leaning, crossed legs and upright sitting. — [Purdue news](https://www.purdue.edu/uns/html4ever/001103.Tan.smartchair.html). A review describes the same PCA-based system, with 10 postures, as 96% subject-dependent and 79% subject-independent. **(secondary; the search excerpts did not show which related-work section stated it)** — [Capacitive smart chair paper (PMC10343373)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10343373/); [MDPI Sensors 22(15):5585](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/15/5585/html)
- Mutlu et al., UIST '07: chair pressure sensors placed with a near-optimal placement strategy. Accuracy was 87% for 10 postures with 31 sensors in cross-validation, and 78% with 19 sensors in a physical deployment, using subject-invariant features on untrained subjects. — [CMU RI](https://ri.cmu.edu/publications/robust-low-cost-non-intrusive-sensing-and-recognition-of-seated-postures)
- Meyer et al. built a textile pressure mat with 96 seat elements and 1 backrest element. With 16 static postures, Naïve Bayes and 16 SFS-selected features, accuracy was about 82%. **(secondary)** — [Sensors 22(15):5585 PDF](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/aebdab2e-6566-4f59-83da-36a6f2860e27/sensors-22-05585-v2.pdf)
- eCushion (Xu et al.): a 16×16 textile array with an Arduino, Bluetooth and a smartphone app. For 7 postures it reached 92% subject-dependent and 79% subject-independent. Another review gives about 86% for 7 postures. **(secondary)** — [Capacitive smart chair paper, related work (PMC10343373)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10343373/); [Sensors 22(15):5585 PDF](https://air.uniud.it/retrieve/aebdab2e-6566-4f59-83da-36a6f2860e27/sensors-22-05585-v2.pdf)
- Roh et al., *Sensors* 18(1):208 (2018), Korea: four low-cost load cells under the seat plate only, using body-weight-ratio features. For 6 postures (one with backrest load), an SVM with RBF kernel gave 97.20% average and 97.94% maximum accuracy on 9 subjects, after a 15-subject preliminary test. **(validation scheme not confirmed)** — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/1/208); [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/s18010208)
- Odesola et al., *Sensors* 25(18):5610 (2025): an office chair with two 32×32 pressure mats (seat and backrest) and **19 postures, including trunk rotation left and right (SP18/SP19)**. The model was trained on a *single individual* as a deliberate "user-tailored" design. CNN accuracy was 98.29% (also tested: DT, RF, SVM, KNN). The authors built a "SitWell" platform for posture duration analytics and sitting-quality assessment. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/18/5610); [PMC12473441](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12473441/); [USW record](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/en/publications/a-smart-system-for-continuous-sitting-posture-monitoring-assessme/)
- An eight-sensor smart chair (Sensors 2022) reached a best accuracy of 91.68% across 8 postures with an echo-state/echo-memory network. — [PMC9371131](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9371131/)
- Pressure array plus infrared array ("privacy-preserving", office workers): 90.6% accuracy with leave-subject-out validation on 21 subjects. — [Adv. Eng. Informatics (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1474034622001501)
- A comparative study of real-time pressure-sensor systems found that cross-subject accuracy "greatly suffers" compared with subject-dependent settings, and tested transfer learning to reduce the drop. — [J. Electrical Engineering 2023](https://reference-global.com/article/10.2478/jee-2023-0055)
- Sensors under the chair legs: one study reported 94% accuracy for familiar users and 72% for unfamiliar users. — [World Scientific](https://worldscientific.com/doi/10.1142/9789811223334_0129)

**Crossed legs**
- A 2024 preprint (with a peer-reviewed version in *Information* 15(12):810) used a 16×16 seat pressure sensor (256 cells, 600 mm square) to classify three states: no crossing, right leg over left, and left leg over right. It tested seven classifiers on only 4 male subjects. The final accuracy did not appear in the excerpts. The authors frame the system as a privacy-friendly alternative to cameras. — [Preprints 202411.0103](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202411.0103); [MDPI Information](https://www.mdpi.com/2078-2489/15/12/810/html)
- Korean conference systems using seat weight distribution classify 1 normal and 6 abnormal postures, including lateral leaning and leg crossing, and push an app alert when an abnormal posture persists. One 2023 cushion uses two air-pressure cells and an IMU and streams over Bluetooth. These were seen as search summaries only; the excerpts did not tie each claim to a specific paper. — [KoreaScience CFKO201725864428816](https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO201725864428816.pdf); [KoreaScience JAKO202331972853771](https://koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO202331972853771.pdf)

**Reviews and field evidence**
- Kappattanavar et al., *JMIR Biomedical Engineering* (2021), systematic review: 14 papers included out of 952. Pressure sensors were the most common sensors, and neural networks the most common classifiers (6 of 14). Only 2 studies took place in free-living settings, and most had ethical or methodological shortcomings. The authors recommend personalized analysis and annotated free-living datasets. — [JMIR Biomed Eng](https://biomedeng.jmir.org/2021/1/e21105)
- "chair&app" smart chair, Belgian office workers (n=28): free-living sitting time agreed with camera at ICC 0.74 and with activPAL over 3 days at ICC 0.89. *Sitting interruptions* agreed poorly with activPAL (ICC 0.38). **(search excerpt)** — [Research Portal Belgium](https://researchportal.be/nl/node/5523806)
- Ryde et al. (2012) sitting pad, n=13: in free-living conditions, agreement with camera was 0.999 for sitting time and 0.997 for transitions. activPAL reached 0.990 and 0.928; ActiGraph performed poorly. — [IJBNPA 9:119 PDF](https://0-ijbnpa-biomedcentral-com.brum.beds.ac.uk/counter/pdf/10.1186/1479-5868-9-119.pdf); [Stirling record](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/20630?mode=full)
- An electro-textile chair cover was tested subject-independently and across different chairs, with 36 subjects, but each recording lasted only 10 s. — [Complex & Intelligent Systems 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40747-026-02460-8)

### Inferences
- For one user, a personal model trained on the user's own labelled sessions sits in the ~92–98% band reported for subject-dependent or single-user models. Unseen-person accuracy is about 78–91%. Prescribed lab postures are exaggerated, though, so real-world accuracy on subtle habitual asymmetry will be lower. No field accuracy figure for posture classes was found.
- "Leaning left/right", "slumped vs upright" and "crossed legs" are the classes with the most supporting data. Asymmetries the user feels but does not produce on purpose (e.g., "left buttock further back") are better tracked as continuous features (L/R ratio, CoP, peak offset) than as classes.
- Sitting duration and break detection from a seat sensor are well validated (ICC 0.89–0.999). Detecting *interruptions or fidgeting* is much weaker (ICC 0.38 in chair&app).

### Gaps
- No multi-week office field study reporting *posture-class* accuracy against video was found.
- No study found that quantifies fidgeting or micro-movement from seat pressure against a video criterion.
- Crossed-leg accuracy numbers from the 2024 study were not visible.
- The original Meyer and Tan papers were not retrieved; their figures are secondary.

## Q3. Backrest pressure/contact sensing: can it quantify which side of the back touches the backrest and trunk rotation?

### Takeaway
Measuring left-vs-right backrest *contact* is simple and has been done in prototypes and patents. Only one study found includes "trunk rotation L/R" as a backrest-plus-seat classification target, and it used a single-user model. No validated *continuous* measure of trunk rotation from backrest pressure was found. Backrest mats can document "only the left back touches" as a contact-area or force ratio, which is a usable private-record metric, but not as a rotation angle.

### Cited Findings
- Odesola et al. (2025) used a 32×32 backrest mat plus a 32×32 seat mat. Their 19 classes included "Rotating the trunk (Left)" and "(Right)". The model was single-user, with 98.29% overall CNN accuracy and no per-class figure retrieved. — [MDPI Sensors 25(18):5610](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/18/5610)
- *Sensors* 26(16):5120 (2026): pressure sensors on the seat and backrest classified 10 seated postures. The most important single sensor (S1) was in the **thoracic region of the backrest**. — [doi 10.3390/s26165120](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26165120)
- Care-Chair (Kumar et al., SMARTCOMP 2016): only 4 pressure sensors, all on the backrest, classified 19 sedentary activities at 86% with 5 users. The authors also claim breathing-rate measurement in static postures. — [Scholars' Mine](https://scholarsmine.mst.edu/comsci_facwork/636); [ORNL record](https://impact.ornl.gov/en/publications/care-chair-sedentary-activities-and-behavior-assessment-with-smar/)
- Zemp, Taylor & Lorenzetti, "Seat pan and backrest pressure distribution while sitting in office chairs", *Applied Ergonomics* 53:1–9 (2016), doi 10.1016/j.apergo.2015.08.004. Only the citation was confirmed; the results were not retrieved. — [citing reference list (Herman Miller page)](https://www.hermanmiller.com/research/categories/white-papers/the-art-and-science-of-pressure-distribution/)
- Herman Miller white paper (manufacturer): in good pressure distribution, peak pressure sits under the sitting bones when upright and moves to the lumbar and thoracic areas when reclined. — [Herman Miller](https://www.hermanmiller.com/research/categories/white-papers/the-art-and-science-of-pressure-distribution/)
- Patents: a "Method for detecting a rotation of part of the trunk of an occupant seated on a seat" (automotive); and interdigital capacitive backrest sensors at the lumbar, shoulder-blade and gluteus-medius contact areas. These are patents, not validations. — [USPTO 12630082](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12630082); [USPTO 12496512 seat system](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12496512)
- An open-source ESP32 smart chair (qppd/smart-chair) uses backrest flex sensors. It compares readings against a per-user baseline and flags lateral lean when left-right asymmetry passes a threshold. This is a demo with no validation. — [GitHub qppd/smart-chair](https://github.com/qppd/smart-chair)
- The review limit applies here too: pressure alone cannot measure spinal or trunk angle. — [Smart Sensing Chairs review](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202403.1695)

### Inferences
- Two simple metrics, a left/right backrest contact-area ratio and a left/right backrest force ratio, would directly record the user's report that "only the left back touches". Combined with the seat L/R ratio, they help separate a whole-trunk lateral lean (seat and backrest shift together) from a trunk twist (backrest asymmetric while the seat stays symmetric). This decomposition is an untested hypothesis, not a validated method.
- Backrest contact depends heavily on backrest shape, recline angle and lumbar-support position. A backrest mat therefore needs to stay on the *same chair with the same settings* for any trend to be meaningful.

### Gaps
- No validation was found of backrest pressure against motion-capture trunk axial rotation (degrees), and no per-class accuracy for trunk-rotation classes.
- Zemp 2016 *Applied Ergonomics* results (backrest pressure by posture) were not retrieved.

## Q4. Load cells under chair legs or seat (bathroom-scale cells, HX711): L/R and F/B weight distribution, precision, studies

### Takeaway
Four load cells, under the seat plate or under the legs, can measure L/R and F/B weight distribution, and they are well validated for that purpose. An instrumented workstation showed errors under 2% of body weight with participants and under 5% with rigid bodies. Roh et al. (Korea) achieved 97% classification of 6 postures with 4 seat-plate cells, and their 2019 follow-up checked the system against force plates. With cells under the chair legs, accuracy drops: about 84% for 7 classes, and 72% for unfamiliar users. Load cells give whole-body CoP and L/R ratios. They cannot resolve individual ischial tuberosities, backrest contact or the shape of contact. They also need periodic calibration.

### Cited Findings
- Roh et al. (2018), described in detail in Q2: four low-cost load cells mounted only on the seat plate, read through an Arduino. Body-weight-ratio features gave 97.20% average accuracy for 6 postures in 9 subjects. Two Kistler 9260AA force plates measured whole-chair and foot loads. **(secondary for the force-plate detail)** — [MDPI Sensors 18(1):208](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/18/1/208)
- Roh, Hyeong & Kim, *International Journal of Industrial Ergonomics* 74:102837 (2019): the 4-load-cell monitoring system was compared with a pressure-distribution system and force plates. The authors report "strong correlations among three instruments' results" and call the results highly reliable. A secondary summary gives 90.3% concordance of body-weight ratios across postures. **(secondary; correlation coefficients and n not retrieved)** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169814117305218); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336964434_Estimation_of_various_sitting_postures_using_a_load-cell-driven_monitoring_system)
- Moriguchi, Sato & Coury, *Safety and Health at Work* 10(3):314–320 (2019): a workstation that continuously records weight-bearing at the seat, backrest, work surface and floor, using load cells of 50–100 kg capacity sampled at 20 Hz.
  - Rigid-body repeat variation was under 0.050 kg, with validity errors within 5%.
  - With participants, errors were under 2% of body weight.
  - It detected posture changes (p=0.007).

  — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/3c8956cafb07441bb93f163dbc951d50); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S209379111830146X)
- Cheng, Zhou, Sundholm & Lukowicz (UBICOMM 2013), DFKI: four pressure sensors under the chair legs. Accuracy was 0.839 for 7 posture classes with 5 subjects and 0.853 for 5 minor activities such as typing; larger activities (office work, meals) were also distinguishable. — [DFKI publication 7002](https://www.dfki.de/web/forschung/projekte-publikationen/publikation/7002)
- "When Chairs Speak" (ACM, 2026): load cells under the chair base with an HX711 amplifier. CoP is derived from left/right and front/back differences between sensors. No accuracy validation appeared in the excerpt. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3802974.3809422)
- A 2019 *Sensors* paper notes that load cells "may provide accurate results, but it required periodic and accurate calibration." — [MDPI Sensors 19(3):455](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/19/3/455)
- Pinupong et al. used a "real-time weight distribution sensor" to compute a buttock L/R ratio (1.16→1.09 after biofeedback). — [TJE](https://he01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/TJE/article/view/264637)

### Inferences
- Load cells under the chair legs also pick up foot, armrest and backrest-recline loading, and the chair's centre of mass shifts as it reclines or swivels. Cells under the *seat plate*, as in Roh, isolate seat load better. For an off-the-shelf office chair, a rigid plate with four cells *on top of* the seat (a "scale cushion") is the least invasive way to copy Roh's geometry. This is an inference; no study of that exact configuration was found.
- The geometry is the same as a Wii-Balance-Board-style CoP. ML CoP ≈ (right cells − left cells) / total × half the sensor spacing, and the L/R ratio follows directly. With 4 cells there is no ischial-peak resolution, so the "left buttock further back" offset cannot be separated from a general backward weight shift.

### Gaps
- No primary source found reports the noise floor, drift or creep of HX711 with half-bridge "bathroom-scale" cells in a seated application (e.g., mm of CoP resolution, or drift over an 8-hour day).
- Correlation coefficients and sample size for the Roh 2019 force-plate validation were not retrieved.

## Q6. DIY builds (Velostat/piezoresistive arrays, FSR grids, load cells with Arduino/ESP32): calibration, drift/creep, hysteresis, accuracy; streaming to a Mac

### Takeaway
DIY piezoresistive mats work for *relative* mapping. One low-cost mat validated against MatScan in 30 sitters had excellent test-retest ICC (0.93–0.98) but only moderate-to-strong agreement with the reference (ρ 0.67–0.70) and a 22% coefficient of variation in total force. Velostat and FSRs have well-documented cyclic and thermal drift, creep and sheet-to-sheet variation, and the creep depends on drive voltage. Expect usable L/R and CoP *trends* after per-session zeroing and a fixed settling time, but not absolute pressure. To stream to a Mac: USB serial (ORSSerialPort) or BLE notify (CoreBluetooth, Nordic-UART-style service).

### Cited Findings
**Validated low-cost mat**
- Jarumethitanont et al., *Scientific Reports* (2024), Mahidol University: a low-cost "Smart mat" compared with Tekscan MatScan. 30 healthy volunteers did three 60-second sitting trials on each mat.
  - Reliability: ICC 0.93 for normalised total force and 0.98 for normalised ischial width.
  - Variability: the coefficient of variation for total force was 22.20%, which the authors attribute to hysteresis and creep.
  - Concurrent validity: Spearman ρ 0.70 for total force and 0.67 for ischial width.
  - Conclusion: usable for assessing force distribution and ischial width.

  — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-72471-3); [Mahidol repository](https://repository.li.mahidol.ac.th/handle/123456789/101352)
- University of Zaragoza thesis (2024): a 16×16 Velostat mat on a 32×32 cm area, built because commercial systems are too expensive for many hospitals and groups. — [Zaragoza TESIS-2024-036](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/131258/files/TESIS-2024-036.pdf?version=1)

**Velostat characterisation**
- A comparative piezoresistive study found Velostat had good hysteresis behaviour but failed the repeatability ANOVA (p>0.05), and it was the least accurate material tested. — [Redalyc](https://www.redalyc.org/journal/430/43050083006/html/)
- Velostat as an in-socket sensor (Imperial College): poor repeatability due to cyclic drift. Hysteresis was defined as the maximum loading/unloading difference as a percentage of full-scale output, tested over 20 cycles at 0–400 kPa. — [Imperial Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/f129aae1-7248-4c9e-8dcc-36aecd017737/content); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339701063_Examination_of_the_Performance_Characteristics_of_Velostat_as_an_In-Socket_Pressure_Sensor)
- A Velostat matrix study reported cyclic drift up to 0.00715 V per cycle and thermal drift causing up to a 67% difference in voltage range. Crosstalk on Velostat matrices has been characterised and quantified separately. **(search excerpt)** — [Crosstalk paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373462247_Characterisation_and_Quantification_of_Crosstalk_on_a_Velostat-Based_Flexible_Pressure_Sensing_Matrix)
- Mechanical reliability: at a 0.5 kg load, output was 3.54±0.06 V as fabricated and 3.8±0.1 V after 200 bending cycles. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/361459122_Investigation_of_the_Mechanical_Reliability_of_a_Velostat-Based_Flexible_Pressure_Sensor)
- Low-cost CoP mats (standing): hysteresis and creep are the main problems and degrade CoP measurement. — [Eng. Proc. 2023 manuscript](https://sciforum.net/paper/download/16586/manuscript)

**FSR characterisation**
- *Materials* (2017), 32 FSR specimens: creep comes from the polymer's rheology (Burger's model plus quantum tunnelling), and the sourcing voltage strongly influences creep response. — [PMC5706281](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5706281/)
- *Dyna* (2018), FlexiForce A201: FSRs show "considerable amounts of hysteresis and repeatability error". A Preisach-model compensation reduced force error compared with the manufacturer's linear fit. — [Dyna / UNAL](https://revistas.unal.edu.co/index.php/dyna/article/view/66432); open data with 16 specimens per model: [Mendeley dataset](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/6zs576k4bc)
- NIME 2006: commercial FSR and touch sensors varied widely in drift and hysteresis. — [IDMIL PDF](https://www-archive.idmil.org/_media/publications/2006/hollinger_2006_nime.pdf)

**Construction notes (forums and student projects; not validated)**
- A typical build places copper row and column strips on either side of Velostat and reads them through a CD74HC4067 multiplexer with 10 kΩ pull-downs. A different Velostat sheet produced only zeros, which the author suspected was due to differing base resistance. — [Fab Academy](https://fabacademy.org/2020/labs/barcelona/students/hala-amer/assignments/week11)
- A 16×16 matrix built without diodes showed crosstalk and noise, and a larger 176×96 build added column diodes to suppress ghosting. — [stm32duino forum](https://www.stm32duino.com/viewtopic.php?p=7148)
- An ESP32 multi-sensor smart-chair repository uses per-user baseline calibration and threshold alerts. — [GitHub qppd/smart-chair](https://github.com/qppd/smart-chair)

**Mac data path**
- ORSSerialPort is a macOS serial-port library for USB-serial devices such as Arduino or ESP32. It opens `/dev/cu.*` paths, sets the baud rate and reads incoming data. — [Swift Package Registry](https://swiftpackageregistry.com/armadsen/ORSSerialPort)
- For BLE, use CoreBluetooth notifications: wait for `.poweredOn`, then scan, connect and subscribe to a notify characteristic. The MTU or payload size limits how much data each packet can carry, so frames must be chunked. — [CoreBluetooth skill notes](https://playbooks.com/skills/nonameplum/agent-skills/corebluetooth); [Apple Developer Forums thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/activities/1587080)

### Inferences
- A defensible DIY design for this user:
  1. **(a)** A 16×16 Velostat or FSR seat mat at roughly 20 mm pitch, comparable to the Sensing Tex dev kit, with diodes or a zero-potential readout to limit crosstalk. Optionally, a coarse 8×8 backrest grid split into left and right halves.
  2. **(b)** Or four half-bridge load cells with HX711 amplifiers under a rigid seat board, which gives better linearity for L/R and CoP but no ischial peaks.
  3. **(c)** An ESP32 streaming at about 10 Hz. A 16×16×1-byte frame is 2.5 kB/s, well within USB serial and feasible over BLE with frame chunking (arithmetic, not from a source).
  4. **(d)** A per-session protocol: tare with nobody seated, wait a fixed settling time, record a short "reference posture" sample, and log relative metrics.

  The cushion settling time of about 6–10 minutes (Q7) and the creep findings argue against comparing absolute values across days without re-zeroing.
- Combining (a) and (b) gives a cross-check: load cells supply true total force and CoP, while the matrix supplies the shape (ischial peaks, contact area). This would partly offset Velostat's poor absolute accuracy (inference).
- For durability, the bending-cycle data are short-term, at 200 cycles. No long-term (months of daily sitting) durability data for DIY seat mats were found.

### Gaps
- No sourced current prices for Velostat sheet, FSR 406/402, HX711 boards or 50 kg half-bridge load cells (Korean retail or otherwise).
- No published accuracy for a DIY Velostat *seat* mat on L/R ratio or CoP against a force plate.
- No data on comfort or perceptibility of DIY mats over long sitting.

## Q7. Limitations: clothing, cushion type, chair mechanics, hysteresis, and confounding between leaning and genuine pelvic asymmetry

### Takeaway
The main threats to validity are:
- sensor creep and hysteresis, which need fixed settling and calibration windows
- cushion material, since viscous foams take longer to settle
- chair mechanics: seat and backrest angle, recline
- the time course of asymmetry within a session
- the fact that a voluntary lean and a structural or habitual pelvic asymmetry produce the same CoP or L/R signal

Pressure cannot measure trunk or spinal angle, and the side that bears more load can be opposite to the pelvic obliquity. So chair sensing can document *that* and *when* asymmetry occurs, but not *why* or *in which anatomical direction*. That fits the app's no-diagnosis principle.

### Cited Findings
- Sensor creep (drift in output under constant load) and hysteresis (readings differ depending on whether load is rising or falling) are inherent to most interface-pressure-mapping technologies. The guidance is to reduce them with low-hysteresis materials and calibration compensation. — [PMG UK Best Practice Guideline BPG2](https://www.pmguk.co.uk/data/page_files/Best%20Practice/BPG2%20Clinical%20Guidelines%20for%20the%20Use%20of%20Interface%20Pressure%20Mapping%20for%20Seating.pdf)
- Air cushions and high-resilience foams settle quickly, while viscous fluids and foams settle slowly. Stinson et al. found that interface-pressure values took roughly 6–10 minutes to stabilise. Creep correction is most accurate when the calibration time window matches the acquisition window. — [Georgia Tech, "The Science of Interface Pressure Mapping"](https://repository.gatech.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6a9b0490-c200-4834-a653-7866f7545477/content)
- Bench-test reliability study of interface pressure (PubMed 14760981): average pressure was reliable. **(search excerpt; details not retrieved)** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14760981/)
- The Mahidol low-cost mat showed excellent ICC but a 22.2% CV in total force, attributed to hysteresis and creep under prolonged load. — [Sci Rep 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-72471-3)
- Vendors' claims of "virtually zero creep" for capacitive mats are marketing material, not independent validation. — [Interface Force blog](https://interfaceforce.co.uk/blog/category/products/pressure-mapping/)
- Common clinical misuse includes positioning a person in an unnatural posture before recording. — [Permobil blog](https://hub.permobil.com/blog/5-common-mistakes-made-when-pressure-mapping-wheelchair-cushions)
- Seat-pan and backrest angles change pressure distribution (Treaster & Marras, SAE 890849). — [SAE](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/seat-pressure-measurement-analysis-890849)
- Pelvic obliquity, windswept hips or scoliosis change seat pressure distribution. — [PMG UK objective measurement document](https://www.pmguk.co.uk/web/data/page_files/From%20OW/Files/Development%20of%20an%20Objective%20Measurement%20System.doc)
- Of 90 SCI patients, 15.5% of those with obliquity loaded the ischial tuberosity on the opposite side more heavily. — [is.muni.cz](https://is.muni.cz/th/g6yex/)
- Pelvic tilt moves CoP in every lean direction (healthy n=28), so a lean and a pelvic shift are coupled in the CoP signal. — [Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/rehabilitation-sciences/articles/10.3389/fresc.2026.1837819/full)
- Asymmetry increases over 30 minutes of seated work in people with unilateral LBP. — [KoreaScience 2014](https://www.koreascience.or.kr/article/JAKO201416760764215.pdf)
- Pressure alone cannot measure trunk or spinal angle. — [Smart Sensing Chairs review](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202403.1695)
- Motion-capture estimates of whole-body centre of mass for wheelchair users, checked against force-plate CoP, agreed within 33 mm AP and 9 mm ML. This shows that even lab-grade methods carry centimetre-level AP uncertainty. — [Chénier et al., J Biomech 2023 (record)](https://isidore.science/document/10670/1.dikpnb)

### Inferences
- Several confounders were not covered by any source found but follow from the mechanics: clothing (wallet or phone in a back pocket, thick seams), cushion swaps, seat-height or recline changes, armrest use, and foot position (feet on a footrest or crossed under the chair). Each can shift L/R or AP CoP by amounts comparable to the asymmetry of interest. The app should log chair settings and a "pockets empty" check, and treat any hardware or cushion change as a new baseline.
- To separate *lean* from *pelvic asymmetry* without a camera, the most promising approach is multi-signal:
  - seat L/R ratio and ischial-peak geometry, together with backrest L/R contact
  - optionally a trunk-worn IMU, or the existing webcam for head and shoulder lean

  A seat-only system cannot make this separation. All of this is inference; no validated decomposition method was found.
- Time-of-day and session-length effects (Hwang 2014) suggest recording asymmetry metrics at fixed times (e.g., the first 10 minutes after settling vs the last 10 minutes of a work block) so that trends are comparable.

### Gaps
- No study found that quantifies the effect of clothing (pocket contents, fabric thickness) on seat CoP or L/R ratio.
- The bench-test error figures quoted in one search summary (≥20% static, 36–51% dynamic) could not be attributed to a specific source page, so they are excluded as unverified.
- No validated method was found for separating voluntary lean from structural pelvic asymmetry using chair sensors alone.
