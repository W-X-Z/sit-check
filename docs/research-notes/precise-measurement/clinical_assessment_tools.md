# Clinical-grade and self-administered tools for periodic, precise static posture / spinal alignment assessment, and their measurement-error benchmarks

Scope note for the report writer: researched 2026-10-11. PMC, Nature, NCBI and Europe PMC full texts were **blocked by the network proxy** (403/DNS), so most numbers below come from search-engine abstracts/extracts of the cited pages (PubMed abstracts, ResearchGate/Academia copies, publisher landing pages). Where an extract was ambiguous or two versions of a source disagree, it is flagged. Evidence-quality tags used: **[SR/MA]** systematic review / meta-analysis; **[Val]** validation against radiograph or 3D lab system; **[Rel]** reliability-only study; **[Small]** n < 30; **[Vendor]** manufacturer/clinic material; **[News]** press. Everything here is framed for measurement and record-keeping, not diagnosis.

## 1. Photogrammetry and posture apps (PostureScreen, APECS, PostureZone, SAPO/PAS, Kinovea + markers): validity, reliability, setup

### Takeaway
Marker-based 2D photogrammetry is reliable for the sagittal craniovertebral angle (CVA). Pooled inter-rater and test-retest ICCs are about 0.89–0.94, and the MDC is about 2.5–5.5°. It is much weaker or unvalidated for thoracic kyphosis, pelvic tilt and frontal-plane asymmetry. Commercial apps (PostureScreen, APECS) have mixed independent evidence: translations are more reliable than angles, and against a Vicon 3D system PostureScreen showed systematic over- and under-estimation. Use them for trend screening, not for accurate values.

### Cited Findings
- **[SR/MA] Photogrammetry-based smartphone apps for spinal posture (Sci Rep 2025/2026).** The PMC-indexed version reports pooled CVA **test-retest ICC = 0.904** and **inter-rater ICC = 0.889**. The Nature landing-page version reports pooled CVA **inter-rater ICC = 0.938 (95% CI 0.852–0.975), 3 studies, total n = 131, I² = 84.82%** (high heterogeneity). **The two versions disagree; check the final version before quoting.** MDCs do not appear to be pooled. — [PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12827935/); [Nature version](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-32708-1)
- **[Rel] ImageJ CVA in people with and without neck pain:** within-day and between-day **ICC 0.89–0.94**; **MDC 2.56°–2.84°**. A companion extract reports **SEM ≈ 0.9°–1.0°** for between-day craniocervical angles. — [PubMed 41509052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41509052/)
- **[Val/Rel] Mobile app for CVA (IJERPH 2020, 17(18):6521):** inter-rater **ICC 0.83–0.89** (mean 0.85); **MDC = 4.96° (intra-rater)** and **5.52° (inter-rater)**. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/18/6521); [PMC7559098](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7559098/)
- **[Rel] Photogrammetric CVA, swayback and knee hyperextension in university students:** inter- and intra-examiner **ICC 0.98**. — [PubMed 40171184](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40171184/)
- **[Rel] 275 Portuguese adolescents, photographic upper-body posture:** intra-rater ICC **0.83 sagittal head angle, 0.78 shoulder angle, 0.66 cervical angle**. This shows that "shoulder" and "cervical" photo angles are less reliable than the head angle. — [Academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/69036787/Intrarater_and_Interrater_Reliability_of_Photographic_Measurement_of_Upper_Body_Standing_Posture_of_Adolescents)
- **[Rel] PostureScreen Mobile, Szucs & Brown 2018 (J Phys Ther Sci):** intra-rater **ICC 0.71–0.99**. Inter-rater reliability was good to excellent for **translations**, and ICCs were stronger for translations than for **angulations**. The authors report "preliminary" construct validity. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322729832_Rater_reliability_and_construct_validity_of_a_mobile_application_for_posture_analysis); vendor summary at [postureanalysis.com](https://www.postureanalysis.com/knowledge-base/rater-reliability-and-construct-validity-of-posturescreen/) **[Vendor]**
- **[Rel] Earlier PostureScreen studies:** Boland et al. reported inter-rater **ICC 0.26–0.93** across 11 variables. Kan et al. reported inter-rater **ICC −0.12 to 0.96** across two sessions. — cited within [Szucs & Brown, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322729832_Rater_reliability_and_construct_validity_of_a_mobile_application_for_posture_analysis)
- **[Val] PostureScreen vs Vicon 3D motion capture (Hopkins, BYU thesis):** PostureScreen **overestimated frontal-plane tilt and shift** and **underestimated sagittal shift**. The author concluded it should not be used where accurate assessment is needed and is suitable only as a screening tool. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Validity-of-PostureScreen-Mobile%C2%AE-in-the-of-Posture-Hopkins/94e1c3c4a35ac7b059a382f9627e69df0f6a874f)
- **APECS:** the only reliability figure found (ICC 0.71–0.99) traces back to a garbled citation of the PostureScreen paper and could not be verified. **No independent APECS validation was found.** — [ResearchGate (Szucs & Brown)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322729832_Rater_reliability_and_construct_validity_of_a_mobile_application_for_posture_analysis)
- **[Val] SAPO/PAS (free Brazilian software, Ferreira et al., Clinics 2010):** on a marked inanimate object the error was **~0.11° for angles and ~1.8 mm for distances**. Inter-rater agreement was very good or excellent for **75% of variables** and intra-rater for **~65% (64.8%; another source says 68.4%)**. Four variables had ICC < 0.70. Reviewers note that SAPO's validity was tested in a single study and that the **lack of standardised anatomical landmarks and angle definitions** undermines photogrammetry in general. — [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/clin/a/pyLZn49HyjSXmJBtBrHqSYF/?lang=en); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Postural-Assessment-Software-(PAS-SAPO):-Validation-Ferreira-Duarte/f6ccc527689ee088f18bd23de0f5331de83cbd58); [IJDR review PDF](https://www.journalijdr.com/sites/default/files/issue-pdf/17892.pdf)
- **[Val][Small] Kinovea vs radiographic Cobb (n = 18, Iranian Rehabil J):** thoracic kyphosis correlated with Cobb **only for experienced raters (r = 0.48 and 0.59)**, and lumbar lordosis was **not significant**. Reliability was nonetheless high: intra-group **ICC 0.90 for kyphosis and 0.91 for lordosis**; inter-group **0.81 and 0.87**. The authors conclude that validity depends on examiner skill. — [IRJ](https://irj.uswr.ac.ir/browse.php?a_id=1319&sid=1&slc_lang=en&html=1)
- **[Val] Kinovea for pelvic kinematics in standing and 45° hip-flexion sitting:** a validation exists (2024/2025), but its numbers were not retrievable. — [PMC11723398](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11723398/)
- **[Val] Kinect depth sensor:** valid for thoracic kyphosis but **not for lumbar lordosis**. — [PMC10024834](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10024834/)

### Inferences
- High reliability is not the same as validity. Kinovea shows ICC ≈ 0.9 but weak radiographic correlation, so a photo protocol can repeat its own bias consistently. For a personal tracker this is acceptable for **within-person trends** if the protocol is identical each time. It does not support claims of absolute values such as "your kyphosis is 48°".
- The sagittal CVA from a side-view photo with tragus and C7 markers is the best-supported self-administered photo metric. It is exactly the metric the frontal webcam cannot capture, so a **periodic side-view photo check-in** (phone on a tripod, fixed distance and height, skin markers) is the most evidence-backed complement to continuous webcam monitoring.
- The app should treat translations (head shift, shoulder height difference in mm or px) as more repeatable than angles, in line with the PostureScreen findings.

### Gaps
- Full texts were unavailable, so I could not extract a pooled MDC or the standard setup parameters (camera distance, lens height, plumb line, marker set) from the 2025 photogrammetry SR. Typical protocols use a camera 2–3 m away at mid-body height with a plumb line and markers on the tragus, C7, acromion, ASIS/PSIS and greater trochanter, but **no source retrieved in this session confirms specific numbers**.
- **PostureZone:** no peer-reviewed validation found.
- No photogrammetry study validated **pelvic tilt (ASIS–PSIS photo angle) against radiographs** in this search. See Q2 and Q7 for why landmark-based pelvic tilt is problematic.

## 2. Spinal shape devices: Spinal Mouse (IDIAG M360), flexicurve, inclinometers and smartphone inclinometers

### Takeaway
For sagittal thoracic kyphosis, skin-surface devices are reliable (ICC about 0.85–0.99) and correlate moderately to strongly with radiographs (r about 0.7–0.9), but they show bias and wide limits of agreement against X-ray. The 2025 meta-analysis recommends the analog inclinometer, flexicurve angle and smartphone apps for clinical use. Smartphone-inclinometer kyphosis has an MDC95 of about 7°, so smaller month-to-month changes are noise. Pelvic tilt by inclinometer (PALM) is reliable, but agreement with radiographic tilt is weak (r ≈ 0.5). Frontal-plane Spinal Mouse measures are only moderately reliable.

### Cited Findings
- **[SR/MA] Nepomuceno et al. 2025 (Braz J Phys Ther):** 72 studies and 15 instruments; 62 studies (86%) rated high quality. Correlation with the radiographic gold standard was strong to moderate, and intra- and inter-rater reliability was excellent. The **Debrunner kyphometer and flexicurve index showed only moderate concurrent validity** vs X-ray. Bland-Altman results were reported for 9 instruments. Recommended for clinical use: **analog inclinometer, flexicurve angle and smartphone apps**. The flexicurve angle **underestimates kyphosis vs X-ray with wide limits of agreement**. — [PubMed 40780025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40780025/); [PMC12355107](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12355107/); [RBF-BJPT](https://www.rbf-bjpt.org.br/en-validity-reliability-clinical-usefulness-instruments-articulo-S1413355525000759)
- **[SR] Barrett et al. 2014 (Man Ther):** validity of non-radiographic kyphosis methods ranged from low to very high; reliability ranged from high to very high. The strongest reliability evidence was for the **Debrunner kyphometer, Spinal Mouse and flexicurve index**. No meta-analysis was performed. — [PubMed 24246907](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24246907/)
- **[Val] Manual inclinometer vs Cobb:** mean difference **4.8° ± 8.9°**, rated good agreement and recommended over the flexicurve. — [PubMed 29111847](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29111847)
- **[Rel] Smartphone inclinometer, standing with arms raised (n = 50):** thoracic kyphosis **ICC 0.92**; **MDC95 = 7.00° for thoracic kyphosis, 9.95° for lumbar lordosis**. — [PubMed 39845224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39845224/)
- **[Rel] Smartphone inclinometer vs flexicurve (n = 60):** both had good intra- and inter-rater reliability (ICC ≈ 0.945–0.964), but **agreement between them was poor (mean difference 19.81° ± 2.8°)**. The two methods are not interchangeable. — [PMC10081858](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10081858/)
- **[Val] Smartphone integrated inclinometer for thoracolumbar kyphosis (n = 100):** mean difference vs PACS **0.4°, limits of agreement −2.4° to +3.2°**. Different spinal region; whether this was measured on the patient or on images needs checking in the full text. — [PMC9130239](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9130239/)
- **[Val][Small] Spinal Mouse vs full-spine radiography (n = 20, Tehran):** **r = 0.81 for thoracic kyphosis, r = 0.86 for lumbar lordosis**; intra-tester ICC 0.92 and 0.89; inter-tester ICC 0.79 and 0.87. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339988445_The_validity_and_reliability_of_spinal_mouse_device_in_measuring_angle_values_of_thoracic_kyphosis_and_lumbar_lordosis). **Contradicted in part by** another comparison that reported Spinal Mouse validity of **0.73** vs 0.81–0.89 for other methods and called its correlation "not trustable" in some studies — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/270875926_Comparing_the_Validity_of_Non-Invasive_Methods_in_Measuring_Thoracic_Kyphosis_and_Lumbar_Lordosis); and by a 2022 JCDR paper stating that sagittal validity "has not been established yet" — [JCDR PDF](https://jcdr.net/articles/PDF/16474/53685_final_F%5BSK%5D_PF1(SC_SS)_PFA(SC_KM)_PN(KM).pdf)
- **[Rel] Spinal Mouse, older women with and without hyperkyphosis, 72-hour retest:** **ICC 0.89–0.99; SEM 1.02°–2.06° (hyperkyphosis) and 1.15°–2.22° (normal)**. — [PubMed 29229059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29229059/); [eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/2ns9g1q9)
- **[Rel] Spinal Mouse, asymptomatic female adolescents:** sagittal thoracic and lumbar **ICC 0.867–0.876 (good)**; frontal-plane **ICC 0.591–0.665 (moderate)**. — [archive.org PDF](https://dn720004.ca.archive.org/0/items/the-reliability-of-measurements-with-the-spinal-mouse-device-in-frontal-and-sagi/10.4328ACAM.6201.pdf)
- **Spinal Mouse (Mannion et al. 2004, Eur Spine J 13:122–136):** described as having "particularly low" SEMs in standing. Between-day global sagittal ICCs of **0.73–0.92** are reported in a secondary citation. — [Eur Spine J reliability paper citing Mannion](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-014-3664-5)
- **[Rel] PALM palpation meter, standing pelvic tilt:** intra-examiner **ICC 0.923–0.935**; pelvic height difference ICC 0.824–0.832. — [Athletic Training & Sports Health Care 2013](https://doi.org/10.3928/19425864-20130417-01). Hagins 1998: intra-tester ICC 0.98 sagittal and 0.84 frontal (as reported in the search extract).
- **[Val] PALM vs standing AP pelvic radiograph (JOSPT 2003):** pelvic crest height difference **ICC 0.90**; leg-length discrepancy **ICC 0.76–0.78** (less accurate). — [JOSPT](https://www.jospt.org/doi/pdf/10.2519/jospt.2003.33.6.319)
- **[Val] PALM pelvic tilt vs lateral radiographs (Spine J 2016 abstract):** anterior-to-posterior tilt **excursion** correlated only **r = 0.509** with radiographic measures. — [Spine J](https://www.thespinejournalonline.com/article/S1529-9430(16)30587-3/fulltext)
- **[Val] Smartphone goniometer (Clino) pelvic tilt vs PALM (Korean study):** **r = 0.83 standing, r = 0.89 sitting**. This is validated against the PALM, not against X-ray. — [KoreaMed](https://www.koreamed.org/SearchBasic.php?RID=2442072)

### Inferences
- A practical self or partner protocol is a smartphone inclinometer placed at T1–T2 and T12–L1 (kyphosis) and at sacrum/L1–L5 (lordosis). It should be treated as a trend metric with an MDC of about 7–10°. A change under about 7° in thoracic kyphosis between monthly check-ins should be logged as "within measurement error".
- The Spinal Mouse gives the best documented between-day SEM (about 1–2°), so it is a reasonable clinic-side periodic measure if a local physiotherapy or chiropractic clinic owns one. Its frontal-plane output should not be used to track left–right asymmetry.
- Self-measurement of thoracic kyphosis with a phone is physically hard because the device sits on one's own back. A partner, or a phone fixed to a wall-mounted guide, is needed. This is an inference; no study of solo self-measurement was found.

### Gaps
- Exact Mannion 2004 SEM values (paper not retrievable).
- Validated Spinal Mouse **MDC** values; only SEMs were found.
- No study found of a **smartphone inclinometer measuring ASIS–PSIS pelvic tilt against radiographs**.
- IDIAG M360 pricing and Korean availability were not found.

## 3. Surface topography / rasterstereography (DIERS formetric, other scanners) and consumer 3D scanning (iPhone LiDAR/TrueDepth)

### Takeaway
DIERS formetric is radiation-free and reliable for sagittal parameters (ICC > 0.90) and pelvic tilt. Its trunk and pelvic **torsion/rotation outputs are the least reliable** (ICC about 0.5–0.6), and its scoliosis estimates **systematically differ from radiographic Cobb angles** (underestimation of about 3.5° to 18° depending on the study). It is a tracking and screening tool anchored to X-ray, not a substitute for it. Consumer iPhone LiDAR/TrueDepth has **no validation for torso asymmetry or scoliosis**. Dedicated smartphone AI back-scan apps show promise in supervised use (r ≈ 0.92, MAE ≈ 6°) but poor reliability when patients scan themselves.

### Cited Findings
- **[SR] Mohokum et al. 2015, "The Validity of Rasterstereography: A Systematic Review":** 12 studies (another listing says 19 eligible); "the accuracy of this method varies". — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/282249013_The_Validity_of_Rasterstereography_A_Systematic_Review)
- **[SR] Surface-topography scoliosis measurements (2022):** reliability and validity review that uses radiographic Cobb as the gold standard. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/365813825_Reliability_and_Validity_of_Scoliosis_Measurements_Obtained_with_Surface_Topography_Techniques_A_Systematic_Review)
- **[MA] Meta-analysis of rasterstereographic validity and reliability (2020):** exists, but its numbers could not be retrieved. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/340562089_Meta-analysis_of_the_validity_and_reliability_of_rasterstereographic_measurements_of_spinal_posture)
- **[Val] Kumar et al. 2016, DIERS 4D vs plain radiographs in idiopathic scoliosis:** the DIERS scanner averaged a Cobb angle **3.49° lower** than radiographs. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1055/s-0036-1583055)
- **[Val] Formetric 4D vs EOS in juvenile and adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (n = 192, Eur Spine J 2019):** mean Cobb **15° (rasterstereography) vs 33° (radiography); mean difference 18°; r = 0.55**. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-018-05876-0)
- **[Val] DIERS vs radiographs (n = 85, Eur Spine J 2025):** significant differences for Cobb angle and other parameters; Bland-Altman analysis showed a **systematic bias**. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-025-09076-5)
- **[Rel] Intra- and inter-day DIERS reliability (n = 50):** ICC **0.734–0.971 except torsion and rotation**; **trunk and pelvic torsion ICC as low as 0.515–0.598**. The authors attribute this to patient positioning and to the fact that these values are calculated rather than directly measured, and recommend the averaged ("Average D") mode. — [PMC3684097](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3684097/)
- **[Rel] Video rasterstereography, 20 healthy volunteers, same-day / between-day / between-week (Eur Spine J 2015):** sagittal parameters (pelvic tilt, kyphosis, lordosis, trunk inclination) had **ICC > 0.90**. Pelvic torsion was more reliable between days than between weeks. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-014-3664-5)
- **[Rel] DIERS 4D in adults without postural deformities:** protocol stands the subject about **2 m from the device**; the light stripe grid is projected on the bare back. — [PMC7040381](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7040381/)
- **[Vendor/Clinic] Acquisition:** 3D spine reconstruction in **< 60 s**, marker-free. — [Today's Medical Developments](https://todaysmedicaldevelopments.com/article/vertebra-by-vertebra); [IDS case study](https://www.ids-imaging.us/casestudies-detail/items/vertebra-by-vertebra.html)
- **[Val] Handheld SpineScan3D (97 AIS patients, EOS reference):** forward-bend surface metrics had good to excellent intra- and inter-examiner reliability. Correlation was moderate with Cobb and good with the scoliometer. **Sensitivity and specificity were about 62–74%** for moderate-to-severe curves. — [PMC10587198](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10587198/)
- **[Val] AI smartphone 3D surface-topography app (n = 125, single institution, 2025/26):** standing and forward-bend scans vs radiographic Cobb gave **r = 0.922, MAE 5.9° (51 test scans)**. Weaker for severe curves and higher BMI. — [PubMed 41615635](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41615635/)
- **[Val] Prospective validation of a smartphone video-based topography app:** moderate reliability and accuracy when trained staff scanned; **poor reliability and more failed scans when patients scanned themselves**. — [PubMed 41921982](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41921982/)
- **[Val] PCdare (research software registering 3D back scans to biplanar X-ray):** optical scanning often **underestimates** deformity; the spinous-process line tracked internal alignment with a median Cobb difference under 1°. — [PMC12728027](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12728027/)
- **[Val] iPad Pro LiDAR, limb lengths only (Sports 2024):** R² 0.901 (thigh) and 0.830 (shank) vs manual, but **only half of the scans were complete**; the torso was not studied. — [doi:10.3390/sports12040092](https://doi.org/10.3390/sports12040092)
- **[Val] iPhone 12 Pro LiDAR:** millimetre-level accuracy within 5 m in a general study, but accuracy for many apps "not yet fully validated". — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/358080588_Human_body_measurement_with_the_iPhone_12_Pro_LiDAR_scanner)
- **[SR] Smartphone and tablet 3D scanners for anthropometry (2025):** good reliability and accuracy, mostly for body composition and circumferences. — [PubMed 41351011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41351011/)
- **Korea:** no Korean clinic list for DIERS formetric was found in Korean-language searches. The Korean Medicine Clinical Practice Guideline blog on scoliosis mentions the scoliometer and ultrasound as radiation-free options; it does not mention rasterstereography. — [NIKOM guideline blog](https://nikom.or.kr/nckm/blog/view.do?menu_idx=65&manage_idx=1&blog_idx=1261)

### Inferences
- If the user can find a clinic with a formetric, its **sagittal and pelvic-tilt outputs** are the parts worth tracking at intervals. Its **torsion and rotation numbers should not be used for trends**, because ICC ≈ 0.5 means day-to-day noise can be as large as the effect. These are the numbers most relevant to the user's "rotation/asymmetry" concerns.
- DIERS Cobb-type estimates must be anchored to the user's own X-ray (a person-specific offset) before they are compared over time. Population-level bias varies from −3.5° to −18°.
- iPhone LiDAR/TrueDepth back scanning for this purpose is **unvalidated**. A self-scan is also the scenario that performs worst even for purpose-built apps. A home 3D-scan feature should be presented as experimental and visual-only, if offered at all.

### Gaps
- Availability and price of DIERS formetric in Korean clinics: **not found**. The device appears to be marketed mainly to private posture, spine and rehabilitation clinics; this is unverified.
- No study was found that validates TrueDepth (front camera) or LiDAR for back-surface asymmetry against radiographs.

## 4. Scoliometer / Adams forward-bend test with a smartphone (e.g., Scoliogauge): validity, thresholds, what it shows and does not show

### Takeaway
The angle of trunk rotation (ATR) in the forward bend is the standard screening measure. An ATR of 5–7° triggers referral for radiographs. ATR measures surface rib or lumbar prominence, which reflects axial rotation; it does not measure the Cobb angle. Leg-length discrepancy can produce a false lumbar prominence, which is why the test is repeated **seated** or with a block under the short leg. Smartphone apps (Scoliogauge and similar) show reliability comparable to a scoliometer, and parents could use one accurately, but specific thresholds for apps are not separately validated.

### Cited Findings
- **Thresholds:** an ATR of **5–7°** is the usual referral threshold for radiographs. — [AAFP 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0101/p19.html); the Korean clinical-guideline blog states that **5–7° or more** warrants suspicion of scoliosis and a radiograph — [NIKOM](https://nikom.or.kr/nckm/blog/view.do?menu_idx=65&manage_idx=1&blog_idx=1261)
- **Sensitivity/specificity (Bunnell scoliometer validation):** a **7° cutoff gives about 83% sensitivity and 86–87% specificity**; a **5° cutoff gives 100% sensitivity but only 47% specificity**. The search extract stated the reference as Cobb > 10°; my recollection of Bunnell (1984) is **Cobb ≥ 20°**. **Verify the Cobb reference before quoting.** — [Bunnell validity PDF (Posturetek)](https://www.posturetek.com/includes/pdf/validity_reliability_testing_scoliometer.pdf); [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/medicine-and-dentistry/scoliometer)
- **[Rel] Scoliometer vs Scoliogauge smartphone app (BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2014):** smartphone-aided ATR showed excellent reliability; reliability of both tools may be influenced by the Cobb angle. — [PMC4198676](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4198676/)
- **[Val] Parents using an inclinometer smartphone app:** reliability and validity were similar to the scoliometer used by professionals. — [PMC9354306](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9354306/)
- **[Val][Small] 3D-printed smartphone holder for ATR (n = 21):** r = 0.976 vs scoliometer. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/366731238_Reliability_and_Validity_of_Angle_of_Trunk_Rotation_Measurement_Using_Smartphone_and_3D_Printing_Technology_in_Scoliosis)
- **Counterpoint:** an earlier paper argued that bare smartphones lack the scoliometer's width and spinous-process notch and "have not been validated as providing reliable clinical measures". It proposed an add-on apparatus. — [PMC4113777](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4113777/)
- **Population self-screening by smartphone:** "The smartphone as a tool to screen for scoliosis, applicable by everyone" (Eur Spine J 2021). Details were not retrieved. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-021-06860-x)
- **What the ATR does and does not indicate:** the scoliometer measures the thoracic **and the lumbar prominence** in the forward bend. A **leg-length discrepancy can cause a false-positive Adams test**. Seated forward bending **eliminates the LLD effect**, and wooden blocks of known height under the shorter leg level the pelvis before testing. — [Pediatric Education](https://pediatriceducation.org/2017/12/04/how-do-you-perform-the-adams-forward-bend-test/); [Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/guide-early-detection-scoliosis); [AO Surgery Reference](https://surgeryreference.aofoundation.org/spine/deformities/adolescent-idiopathic-scoliosis/further-reading/patient-examination)
- **Sacral ATR as an indirect LLD measure:** a registered study adds blocks until the sacral scoliometer reading reaches 0°, then correlates the reading with block height. Results were not retrieved. — [ClinicalTrials NCT04713397](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04713397)
- **Normal asymmetry (children aged 5–18, n = 2071, Grivas et al.):** ATR = 0° in about **65–67% standing** and **75–76% sitting**. ATR ≥ 7° in **3.2% (boys) to 3.9% (girls)** in standing forward bend. Right-sided asymmetry was more common, and **sitting forward bend best reflected true asymmetry**. — [Scoliosis 2006;1:19](https://scoliosisjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1748-7161-1-19); [PMC1693569](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1693569/)
- **Body mass:** thoracic scoliometer readings vary with BMI ("body mass hides the curve"). — [PMC5422115](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5422115/)
- **Adults:** trunk asymmetry becomes prominent after adolescence, per a radiograph study of normal spines. No adult scoliometer norms were found. — [PMC3032747](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3032747/)

### Inferences
- The user's "one side of the low back rises when bending forward" is exactly what a **lumbar ATR** quantifies. A sensible private record is the lumbar ATR in **standing** forward bend **and in seated** forward bend. If the prominence shrinks markedly when seated, that is consistent with (but does not prove) a leg-length or pelvic contribution. The app should record both numbers without interpreting them, and the pending leg-length measurement and X-ray will clarify the cause.
- An ATR value is a screening and trend number, not a diagnosis. A change of 1–2° between sessions is likely within operator and positioning error. No validated MDC was found (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No validated **MDC or SEM for smartphone ATR in adults** was retrieved. Scoliometer reproducibility in the literature is commonly quoted as about ±2°, but this was **not verified this session**.
- No Scoliogauge-specific sensitivity and specificity data.
- No adult normative ATR distribution.

## 5. Radiographic gold standards (whole-spine standing X-ray, EOS): parameters and how home measures can be anchored to them

### Takeaway
A standing whole-spine AP/lateral radiograph, or EOS biplanar low-dose imaging, provides the reference values: coronal Cobb angle(s) (scoliosis is defined as ≥10°), sagittal kyphosis and lordosis, spinopelvic parameters (pelvic incidence PI = pelvic tilt PT + sacral slope SS), sagittal vertical axis, pelvic obliquity, and (on full-length films) structural leg-length discrepancy. Radiographic Cobb has 3–5° measurement error, so a change of ≥5° is the conventional threshold for real progression. EOS is available in Korea at some university and spine hospitals, with roughly one tenth the dose of conventional X-ray (hospital claims).

### Cited Findings
- **Scoliosis definition:** coronal Cobb angle **≥ 10°**. — [AAFP 2020](https://www.aafp.org/pubs/afp/issues/2020/0101/p19.html)
- **Cobb measurement error:** inter- and intra-observer variability is about **3–5°**. It can reach **10° or more** in severe curves and relates to vertebral rotation. **Progression is conventionally defined as a change of ≥ 5°.** — [Springer (Int Orthop 2016)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00264-016-3359-0); [PMC9027061](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027061/); [ScienceDirect (Clin Biomech 1987)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0268003387900398)
- **Software measurement:** intra-observer mean error **1.8° (software) vs 2.31° (manual)**. — [PMC9027061](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027061/)
- **Spinopelvic parameters:** **PI = SS + PT**; theoretical **PT ≈ 0.44·PI − 11°** in asymptomatic people; "ideal" PT < 20°. — [Eur Spine J 2019 "Sagittal balance of the spine"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-019-06083-1)
- **Normal values:** 709 asymptomatic adults: **PI 52.6 ± 10.4°, PT 13.0 ± 6.8°, SS 39.6 ± 7.9°**; the PT 95% range is about −0.6° to 26.7°. — [Vialle et al. PDF](https://www.vertebra.fr/biblio/007.pdf). Brazilian adults (n = 130): **PT 12.4°, PI 49.4°, SVA −0.54 cm** — [PMC6251252](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6251252/). PI-regulated lordosis norms — [PMC3489325](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3489325/)
- **Leg-length discrepancy (PLOS ONE 2021 SR):** **full-length standing AP radiography is the most valid and reliable method** and the reference standard. The **block test** is the most useful clinical method. There is **high-level evidence that the tape measure is less valid and reliable**. — [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0261457)
- **Lift vs tape vs radiograph (J Rheumatol):** **r = 0.93 (lift/blocks) and r = 0.75 (tape)**; the tape had about −5 mm systematic bias. — [J Rheumatol](https://www.jrheum.org/content/41/8/1689)
- **EOS limb length (JBJS 2013, phantom):** mean absolute error **2.6 mm (slow) and 3.6 mm (fast)**, vs 6.3 mm for CT scanogram and **42.2 mm for conventional radiographs**. — [PubMed 24306706](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24306706/)
- **EOS pelvic parameters, phantom with 105 positional variations:** measurement-error SD **0.26° for obliquity**; 3.23° flexion, 2.98° PI, 0.88° spinopelvic tilt, 3.22° SS. — [PMC8129068](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8129068/)
- **EOS pelvic dose:** **8.19 vs 19.38 dGy·cm²** for plain X-ray. EOS has **no magnification**; plain films average 1.15×. — [PubMed 28942025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28942025/)
- **EOS review:** standing biplanar imaging with 3D reconstruction of spine and pelvis. A review notes that NICE acknowledged a dose benefit but did not recommend EOS over standard X-ray (as reported in the search extract). — [J Child Orthop 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11832-016-0713-0); [PMC10261215](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10261215/)
- **[News] EOS in Korea:** Konyang University Hospital announced it was the first in Korea (Dec 2016) — [News1](https://www.news1.kr/local/daejeon-chungnam/2850968), [굿모닝충청](http://www.goodmorningcc.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=52034); Park Won-wook Hospital (Busan, 2018) — [부산일보](http://m.busan.com/m/News/view.jsp?newsId=20180311000243). Hospital-reported dose is about **0.28 mSv vs 2.95 mSv** for conventional X-ray — [의학신문](http://www.bosa.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2167034), [서울경제](https://www.sedaily.com/NewsView/1S3HGZKJ18). A reimbursement change reportedly cut the fee from about 300,000 to about 16,000 KRW (date unclear) — [Medigate](https://m.medigatenews.com/news/3260779105)
- **Korean reference:** Seoul National University Hospital's patient information states that plain radiography is the most important test for scoliosis diagnosis and treatment. — [SNUH](https://www.snuh.org/health/nMedInfo/nView.do?category=DIS&medid=AA000193)

### Inferences
- **Anchoring strategy (after diagnosis):** on the day of the X-ray, or as close to it as possible, the user records the same home measures: side-view CVA photo, smartphone-inclinometer kyphosis, ASIS–PSIS or PALM-style pelvic angle, standing and seated lumbar ATR, and block-test leg-length estimate. Each home metric then gets a **personal offset** relative to the radiographic value. Later home readings are interpreted as change from that baseline, never as absolute radiographic equivalents.
- **Terminology trap:** radiographic "pelvic tilt" (PT, hip axis to sacral endplate vs vertical) is **not the same quantity** as the clinical "anterior pelvic tilt" (ASIS–PSIS angle). By definition a more anteverted pelvis gives a **lower** radiographic PT and higher SS. This is an inference from the definitions above. The app should label metrics precisely ("ASIS–PSIS angle (surface)" vs "PT (radiograph)").
- Because Cobb error is 3–5°, even radiographic changes under 5° are not considered real. Home metrics with larger MDCs cannot detect smaller changes than the gold standard.

### Gaps
- Current (2026) EOS availability list and current fee in Korea: news items are from 2016–2018. HIRA data were not retrieved.
- Whether the user's pending "whole-spine X-ray" will be EOS or conventional stitched films is unknown.

## 6. Measurement-error benchmarks: day-to-day variability, MDCs, and reliability of self-perceived asymmetry

### Takeaway
Habitual standing posture is fairly consistent over a week for most photo variables (ICC 0.9+). The exception is **forward head position in lateral standing, ICC ≈ 0.67 over 7 days**, so head-forward metrics fluctuate the most. Usable "real change" thresholds: CVA about 3–5.5° (MDC); thoracic kyphosis by smartphone inclinometer about 7°; lumbar lordosis about 10°; radiographic Cobb 5°; Spinal Mouse SEM 1–2°. DIERS torsion is too noisy to trend. Self-perceived trunk appearance does **not** correlate with objective asymmetry in scoliosis cohorts, so the user's subjective reports should be logged but not treated as measurements.

### Cited Findings
- **[Rel] One-week consistency of habitual posture (asymptomatic men, days 1, 4 and 7, digital camera, multiple landmarks):** high reliability overall, but **forward head position in lateral standing ICC = 0.67** vs **head angle ICC = 0.92**. Anterior and posterior standing views were highly reliable. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1746068908000217)
- **[Rel] Children, sagittal relaxed standing photographed twice within an hour (n = 38):** **ICC > 0.93**, no systematic retest effect. — [BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2005;6:35](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/1471-2474-6-35)
- **[Rel] Time of day:** natural head position reproducibility is not affected by time of day, but orientation was **more stable at noon and in the evening than in the morning**. — [PMC3536460](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3536460/)
- **MDC and SEM summary from the studies above:** CVA MDC **2.56–2.84°** (ImageJ, [PubMed 41509052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41509052/)); CVA MDC **4.96–5.52°** (app, [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/17/18/6521)); thoracic kyphosis MDC95 **7.00°** and lumbar lordosis **9.95°** (smartphone inclinometer, [PubMed 39845224](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39845224/)); Spinal Mouse SEM **1.0–2.2°** ([PubMed 29229059](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29229059/)); Cobb progression **≥ 5°** ([PMC9027061](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027061/)); DIERS torsion ICC **≈ 0.52–0.60** ([PMC3684097](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3684097/)); DIERS same-day repeat SD within about **±3°** (secondary claim from a search extract, source [PMC4590960](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4590960), unverified)
- **Pelvic landmark asymmetry in normal pelves (Preece et al. 2008):** ASIS–PSIS angle ranged **0–23° (mean 13 ± 5°)**. **Side-to-side differences up to 11° in ASIS–PSIS tilt and 16 mm in innominate height arose from pelvic morphology alone.** — [Salford repository](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1456791/variation-in-pelvic-morphology-may-prevent-the-identification-of-anterior-pelvic-tilt); [J Man Manip Ther](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1179/106698108790818459)
- **Most asymptomatic people show some anterior pelvic tilt** by palpation meter, and normative values vary widely. — [IJSPT review "Clinical Measures of Pelvic Tilt in Physical Therapy"](https://ijspt.scholasticahq.com/article/27978-clinical-measures-of-pelvic-tilt-in-physical-therapy); [PMC8486407](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8486407/)
- **Self-perception vs objective asymmetry:** in 30 adolescent girls with AIS, the self-perceived appearance score (SAQ) showed **no correlation** with photogrammetric trunk-asymmetry indices **ATSI (r = −0.014)**, **POTSI (r = 0.113)** or **Cobb (r = 0.049)**. — [PubMed 42290434](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42290434/). An earlier abstract found TAPS (subjective) vs Cobb **r = −0.55** — [PMC3675416](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3675416/)
- **Seat-pressure perception:** a search extract reports that people do not perceive ischial pressure differences below about **1.9 kPa**. The primary source was not identified (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- **Suggested "real change" rule for the app:** flag a change only if it exceeds the metric's MDC (CVA ≥ 5°, thoracic kyphosis ≥ 7°, lumbar lordosis ≥ 10°, lumbar ATR at least 2–3° pending a validated MDC). Otherwise display "no change beyond measurement error". Use the mean of 3 captures per session and a fixed time of day (midday or evening rather than morning), since averaging reduces error (consistent with the DIERS "Average" mode recommendation).
- Forward-head metrics have the lowest week-to-week stability, so weekly CVA comparisons need at least 3 photos per session and should be interpreted over a multi-week trend.
- Left–right pelvic landmark differences of up to about 11° can be **bony morphology**, not posture. A home ASIS/PSIS asymmetry reading therefore cannot by itself separate structure from habit, which is another reason not to infer a "correction direction" before imaging.
- The user's subjective reports ("only left back touches backrest", "left buttock further back") should be stored as **patient-reported observations**, separate from measurements and never merged into a score.

### Gaps
- No study isolating **true habitual day-to-day variance in adults over many weeks** with a fixed protocol. Existing studies mix measurement error with true variation.
- No adult **MDC for ATR (lumbar)**, **ASIS–PSIS photo angle**, or **seated asymmetry**.
- The source for the 1.9 kPa perception threshold could not be identified.

## 7. Which of the user's complaints have validated measurement methods at all

### Takeaway
- **Well covered:** forward head (CVA, photo), thoracic kyphosis (inclinometer, Spinal Mouse, X-ray), lumbar prominence on forward bend (ATR, scoliometer) and leg-length discrepancy (X-ray, block test).
- **Partly covered:** anterior pelvic tilt. Surface ASIS–PSIS methods are reliable but confounded by bony morphology and correlate weakly with radiographs; radiographic PT/SS are valid but measure a different construct.
- **Weakly covered:** internally rotated or rounded shoulders. Acromion–table distance has ICC 0.91 but is supine; photo "shoulder angle" has ICC ≈ 0.78; no validated standing humeral-rotation metric was found.
- **Poorly covered:** rib flare. The infrasternal angle is reliable but has no normative values; no validated "rib flare" metric exists.
- **Essentially unvalidated:** seated left–right asymmetry. Pressure mats are partially reliable, and there are no validated trunk-rotation-in-sitting metrics for this use.

### Cited Findings
- **Anterior pelvic tilt:** PALM reliable (ICC 0.92–0.98) — [ATSHC 2013](https://doi.org/10.3928/19425864-20130417-01); weak radiographic correlation r = 0.509 — [Spine J 2016](https://www.thespinejournalonline.com/article/S1529-9430(16)30587-3/fulltext); morphology confound — [Preece 2008](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1456791/variation-in-pelvic-morphology-may-prevent-the-identification-of-anterior-pelvic-tilt); DIERS pelvic tilt is a reliable sagittal parameter (ICC > 0.90) — [Eur Spine J 2015](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00586-014-3664-5). A search extract also claimed "high validity" of rasterstereographic pelvic parameters vs EOS in degenerative-disc patients, but the specific paper could not be identified (possibly [Research Square rs-14377](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-14377/v1)), so **treat that claim as unverified**
- **Rounded or internally rotated shoulders:** acromion–table distance (supine) inter-rater **ICC 0.91**, SEM 0.22–0.28% of body length (middle-aged women) — [Physiotherapy 2016](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031940616300384). Sahrmann's ≤ 2.54 cm "normal" pectoralis-minor criterion and a 2.6 cm rounded-shoulder cutoff are used in research — [PMC10390035](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10390035/). The pectoralis minor index (coracoid to 4th rib ÷ height) has a reference value of **7.44%**, but no PMI reliability data was retrieved — [PMC10390035](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10390035/). Pectoralis-minor length test intra-rater reliability and diagnostic accuracy were studied — [BMC Musculoskelet Disord 2007;8:64](https://bmcmusculoskeletdisord.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2474-8-64) (values not retrieved). Photo "shoulder angle" intra-rater **ICC 0.78** — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/69036787/Intrarater_and_Interrater_Reliability_of_Photographic_Measurement_of_Upper_Body_Standing_Posture_of_Adolescents)
- **Rib flare:** infrasternal angle on radiographs: intra-rater **ICC 0.991**, inter-rater **0.993**; **r = 0.85** with goniometer — [J Musculoskelet Sci Technol 2018](https://www.jkema.org/archive/view_article?pid=jkema-2-2-33). Clinical goniometer ISA: right side ICC **0.866 (intra) / 0.813 (inter)**, left side **0.919 / 0.846** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283664327_Intra-_and_Inter-rater_Reliabilities_of_Infrasternal_Angle_Measurement). No normative database and no functional validation — [Brookbush Institute](https://brookbushinstitute.com/articles/rib-flare-assessment-the-myth-of-flared-ribs) **[non-peer-reviewed educational source]**. Practitioner note: arm position (shoulder flexion) tilts the thorax and falsifies the reading — [Medium essay](https://wayne-sun.medium.com/on-infrasternal-angles-and-respiration-e5cf6f881c8c) **[opinion]**
- **Left front ribs protruding / back-side prominence:** the standard surface quantification of rib asymmetry is the **thoracic ATR in forward bend** (scoliometer). See Q4 — [PMC4198676](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4198676/)
- **Seated asymmetry (left buttock further back, only left back on backrest):** pressure-mapping bench tests found **dispersion index, contact area and percent force in the ischial regions reliable**, but **not peak pressure** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8887903_Reliability_of_Bench_Tests_of_Interface_Pressure). Pediatric clinical sitting: weight distribution and lateral centre-of-pressure transfer were repeatable — [Advances in Physiotherapy 2008](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/14038190701256378). A 2026 study measured the left–right ischial-tuberosity pressure difference in older adults with a single test, relying on prior reliability evidence — [ScienceDirect 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0197457226003903). DIERS **pelvic torsion** (the closest proxy for pelvic rotation) is the least reliable parameter (ICC ≈ 0.52–0.60) — [PMC3684097](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3684097/)
- **Right hip stiffer in figure-4:** no source was searched specifically. Hip external-rotation ROM goniometry is a standard clinical measure, but **no validation data was retrieved this session**.
- **Leg-length discrepancy (pending):** radiograph is the reference; block test is the best clinical method — [PLOS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0261457)

### Inferences
- **Recommended periodic (weekly or monthly) self-check-in set, ranked by evidence:**
  1. Side-view photo CVA with skin markers, tripod and fixed distance (best evidence; MDC about 3–5°).
  2. Smartphone-inclinometer thoracic kyphosis with a partner (MDC about 7°).
  3. Standing **and** seated forward-bend lumbar and thoracic ATR with a smartphone scoliometer app (screening-grade; record only).
  4. Back-view photo for shoulder and pelvic height differences in mm (translations are more repeatable than angles).
  5. Optional infrasternal-angle goniometer reading (reliable, but no norms, so trend only).
  6. Patient-reported asymmetry log.
- **Clinic-side periodic options (in order of precision):** EOS or standing full-spine X-ray (reference, radiation, only when clinically ordered) > Spinal Mouse (SEM 1–2°, sagittal only) > DIERS formetric (sagittal and pelvic tilt reliable; torsion unreliable; Cobb biased).
- The user's two most "rotational" concerns (seated asymmetry and left-sided rib/buttock prominence) are the **least measurable** at home. The app should say so explicitly and defer to imaging rather than infer a cause or a correction direction.

### Gaps
- No validated standing measure of **humeral internal rotation** (as distinct from scapular protraction) was found.
- No validated metric for **"rib flare"** as a construct; the infrasternal angle is a proxy without norms.
- No adult reliability or MDC data for **left–right ischial load ratio** on low-cost seat mats. A "low-cost smart mat, ICC 0.93–0.98" result appeared in a search extract **without an identifiable source**, so it is excluded.
- Costs of consumer and clinical tools (Spinal Mouse, PALM, scoliometer, DIERS session fees in Korea) were not retrieved.
